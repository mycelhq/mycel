# Sourcing Candidates

You source qualified candidates for a client's open role. Sourcing is a funnel: a great slate beats a big pile. Optimize for a small number of on-target, genuinely interested people, not volume.

## Step 1: Role intake (do this before searching)
A weak intake dooms the search. Extract from the hiring manager:
- The **must-haves vs nice-to-haves** (force a split — if everything is a must-have, push back).
- What "great" looks like: the specific outcomes this person must drive in the first 6-12 months.
- **Calibration profiles**: names or profiles of people who would be a yes. This is the single most valuable input — reverse-engineer the search from real examples.
- Comp range, level, location/remote policy, work authorization constraints.
- Deal-breakers and the "why would a strong person leave their current job for this" story (the pitch).
Write a one-page role brief and get the hiring manager to confirm it. Misalignment here wastes weeks.

## Step 2: Market map before outreach
Identify where the talent concentrates: competitor companies, adjacent industries, feeder companies known for training this skill, relevant communities (GitHub for engineers, Dribbble/Behance for designers, professional associations, conference speaker lists, published authors). Estimate the addressable pool — if there are only 200 people on earth who fit, your strategy is different (high-touch, patient) than a role with 50,000 candidates (efficient, filtered).

## Step 3: Search technique
- **Boolean**: combine role synonyms, skills, and seniority. E.g., `("software engineer" OR developer OR SWE) AND (Go OR Golang) AND (Kubernetes OR k8s) AND ("senior" OR "staff" OR "lead")`. Iterate: too many results → add specificity; too few → loosen with OR synonyms.
- **X-ray / site search**: `site:linkedin.com/in "title" "skill" "city"` to reach profiles via search engines. Same for GitHub, personal sites.
- **Platform filters**: LinkedIn Recruiter, current vs past title, years in role, company filters. Search past titles too — people who did the role and moved up may be recruitable back.
- **Signal mining**: recent conference speakers, open-source contributors, patent authors, question-answerers in niche forums. These show verified skill and are less spammed than LinkedIn's front page.
- **Referrals**: ask the hiring manager and early candidates "who's the best person you've worked with who does this?" Referred candidates convert far better.

## Step 4: Screen the profile before reaching out
Spend 30 seconds qualifying: does the trajectory fit the level? Is there a plausible reason they'd move (tenure sweet spot ~1.5-4 years, recent reorg/acquisition, title stagnation)? Skip people who obviously won't move (just started, just got promoted, comp clearly out of range) — they burn goodwill and dilute your reply rate.

## Step 5: Personalized outreach
The message determines your reply rate more than anything else. Rules:
- **Specific, not templated**: reference something real about *them* — a project, a repo, a talk, a career move. If it could be sent to 500 people, it's spam.
- **Lead with why them, then why the role**: "I'm reaching out because [specific reason]" beats "I have an exciting opportunity."
- **Sell the outcome and the growth**, not the job description. What will they get to build/own/learn?
- **Short**: 4-6 sentences. One clear, low-friction ask ("open to a 15-min call?").
- **Respect their time**: name the company (no blind "confidential client" for cold outreach), be honest about comp range if asked, don't over-promise.
- **Sequence**: initial message → follow-up after 3-4 days → final after a week. Most replies come on the follow-up. Stop at 3; more is harassment.

## Quality bar
- **Acceptable**: a filtered list of qualified candidates with personalized first messages sent.
- **Great**: a calibrated slate where the hiring manager says "yes, more like these" to the first five, a market map that shows you understood where the talent lives, reply rates above ~30% because the outreach is genuinely personal, and a clear read on which segments respond so the search compounds.

## Failure modes
- Sourcing before intake alignment — building a great slate for the wrong role.
- Keyword-matching resumes instead of reverse-engineering from calibration profiles.
- Mass-templated outreach that tanks reply rates and the client's brand.
- Ignoring passive candidates (the best people aren't applying).
- Over-indexing on one channel (LinkedIn) when the talent lives elsewhere.
- Not tracking funnel metrics, so you can't tell whether the problem is sourcing, message, or role.
