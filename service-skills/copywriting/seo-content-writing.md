# SEO Content Writing

You are writing content that must satisfy two readers: the human searcher who needs a genuine answer, and the search engine deciding whether to rank you. Modern SEO rewards the same thing users want — the most helpful, credible, complete answer to the query. Write for the human; structure for the engine.

## Keyword and intent research first

Never write before you know the target query and its intent. For the primary keyword, determine **search intent** by actually looking at the current top results: are they how-to guides, listicles, product pages, definitions, comparisons? The SERP tells you the format and depth Google already rewards — match it or beat it, don't fight it. Classify intent: informational, commercial (comparison/best-of), transactional, or navigational. Map keywords to funnel stage. Gather the primary keyword plus **semantic/related terms and questions** (from "People Also Ask," related searches, and tools like Ahrefs/SEMrush) — these define topical completeness, not density targets.

## Build for topical authority

Ranking today favors comprehensive topic coverage over isolated keyword pages. Use a **pillar + cluster** model: a broad pillar page covering the topic at depth, linking to specific cluster posts answering sub-queries, all interlinking. This signals expertise on the whole topic. When writing one piece, cover the subtopics a genuine expert would — the questions in PAA and the headings across top competitors reveal the coverage bar. A thin post targeting one keyword loses to a thorough resource.

## Structure the page for skimmers and crawlers

- **One `<h1>`** containing the primary keyword, phrased for the human (compelling, not robotic).
- **Logical `h2/h3` outline** that mirrors how someone researches the topic; work related keywords and question-phrases naturally into subheadings. The outline is the skeleton — write it before prose.
- **Answer fast**: give the core answer high on the page (especially for informational/featured-snippet queries — a concise 40–55 word definition/summary near the top can win the snippet). Don't bury the answer under 300 words of preamble.
- Short paragraphs (2–4 lines), bullet lists, tables for comparisons, bolded key phrases — formatted for the scroller and for rich results.
- Include a table of contents for long guides (jump links, better UX, potential sitelinks).

## Write for E-E-A-T and genuine helpfulness

Google's quality signals reward Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trust. In practice: demonstrate first-hand experience (specifics, examples, "here's what actually happens"), cite credible sources and link out to them, be accurate and current, and show who's behind the content (author bio/credentials where relevant). Avoid the generic AI-filler tone that says nothing — add real specifics, data, examples, and opinions that a novice couldn't write. Content that merely rephrases the top 3 results adds nothing and increasingly won't rank.

## Keyword usage — natural, not stuffed

Use the primary keyword in: the title tag, H1, the intro (first ~100 words), at least one H2, and naturally through the body and conclusion — but at human density. Keyword stuffing is penalized and reads terribly. Rely on semantic variants and related terms for relevance signals; Google understands synonyms and topics, not just exact strings. If a sentence reads awkwardly to fit a keyword, rewrite it — the human always wins.

## On-page SEO elements

Write the **title tag** (50–60 chars, keyword front-loaded, compelling — it's the ad in the SERP) and **meta description** (140–160 chars, benefit + implicit CTA to earn the click). Add descriptive **alt text** to images. Include **internal links** to related cluster/pillar pages with descriptive anchor text, and **external links** to authoritative sources. Suggest schema (`Article`, `FAQPage`, `HowTo`) where the content fits. Slug: short, keyword-based, hyphenated.

## Length and depth

Length follows the query, not a quota. Match or slightly exceed the depth of the ranking competitors — comprehensive, but no padding. A 600-word answer that fully satisfies a simple query beats a padded 2,500-word one. Cut filler ("in today's digital landscape") ruthlessly; every paragraph must add information.

## Quality bar

Acceptable: on-topic, keyword present, decent length. **Great**: intent matched to the actual SERP, topical coverage matching or exceeding competitors and PAA, the answer delivered fast and snippet-ready, E-E-A-T signals present (specifics, sources, experience), keyword usage natural with rich semantic variation, and complete on-page elements (title/meta/alt/internal links/schema). Failure modes to reject: writing before checking the SERP intent, keyword stuffing, thin coverage that rephrases the top results, burying the answer under preamble, generic AI-filler with no real substance, padding to hit a word count, and missing title/meta/internal links. Would an expert bookmark this? If not, it's not done.
