# Email & Nurture Sequences

Email is the highest-ROI channel because it reaches an audience that already opted in — but only if it earns the open, the read, and the click. Every email fights the inbox: a hundred competitors, a two-second glance, a hovering delete finger. Write accordingly.

## One email, one goal

Each email has a single job and a single primary CTA. A welcome email welcomes and sets expectations; a sales email drives one purchase decision; a nurture email delivers one useful idea. Multiple asks split attention and tank clicks. Decide the one action before writing a word, and make everything serve it.

## Subject line and preview text — the whole email depends on them

Nothing else matters if it isn't opened. Subject line rules:

- Front-load the value or curiosity; ~30–50 chars so it survives mobile truncation.
- Specific beats vague ("Your invoice is 12 days overdue" > "Following up"). Curiosity gaps, benefits, urgency (honest), personalization, and numbers all lift opens — but never clickbait that the body doesn't pay off (destroys trust and future opens).
- Avoid spam triggers (ALL CAPS, "FREE!!!", excessive punctuation, "act now") that hit filters.
- Write the **preview/preheader text** deliberately — it's the second line in the inbox and extends the subject. Never leave it as "View in browser" or the first "Hi there."
- A/B test subject lines when volume allows; it's the highest-leverage test in email.

## Structure the body for a two-second scan

Most people skim. Structure: a strong opening line (the first sentence must pull them in — no throat-clearing "I hope this email finds you well"), short paragraphs (1–3 lines), one idea, and a clear CTA. Get to the point fast; respect their time. Use a conversational, one-to-one voice — email is personal, write like a person to a person, not a press release. Keep most emails short; length is earned by value, and nurture/sales emails usually win by being tight.

## The CTA

One primary CTA, stated as value ("Claim your spot," "See the results," "Fix this now"), placed where a scanner will hit it. For longer emails, repeat it. Reduce friction ("takes 30 seconds"). A secondary link is fine but must be clearly subordinate. Make the click the obvious next step.

## Sequences — design the arc, not just single sends

Automated sequences carry the relationship. Design each with a purpose and cadence:

- **Welcome sequence** (on signup): deliver the promised thing immediately, set expectations, establish voice, quick early win, soft intro to the offer. First impression sets engagement for the whole list.
- **Nurture sequence**: alternate genuine value (education, insight, stories) with occasional offers — the 80/20 give/ask rhythm. Build trust before selling. Each email should stand alone yet advance a narrative.
- **Sales/launch sequence**: escalate — problem → solution → proof/testimonials → objection handling → urgency/deadline → last call. The final-day emails often drive the majority of conversions; don't undersell the close.
- **Re-engagement/win-back**: for dormant subscribers — a pattern interrupt, a "we miss you"/"is this goodbye?" honest ask, often a sunset (remove non-openers to protect deliverability).

Map the sequence: number of emails, timing/delays, entry/exit triggers, and the goal of each. Write them as a set that reads coherently, not isolated messages.

## Personalization and segmentation

Use merge fields correctly (and test the fallback — "Hi {FirstName}" showing blank is worse than "Hi there"). Better: segment by behavior/stage and write to that segment's context. Reference what you know (their signup reason, last action). Relevance is the real personalization; a first-name token is the least of it.

## Deliverability and compliance

Copy affects deliverability. Maintain a healthy text-to-link ratio, avoid spam-trigger language and all-image emails, include a genuine plain-text version, and always include a working unsubscribe and physical address (CAN-SPAM/GDPR). Sending relevant content to engaged people is itself a deliverability strategy — filters watch engagement.

## Quality bar

Acceptable: grammatical, on-brand, has a link. **Great**: a subject + preheader pair that earns the open honestly, an opening line that hooks, a scannable body with one idea and one value-stated CTA, sequences architected with purposeful cadence and an escalating arc, real segmentation-driven relevance, and clean deliverability/compliance. Failure modes to reject: multiple competing CTAs, "I hope this finds you well" openers, clickbait subjects the body doesn't pay off, ignored preheader text, walls of text, no unsubscribe, broken merge fallbacks, and sequences that are just disconnected blasts. Read each email on a phone in two seconds — if the value and the action aren't instantly clear, rewrite it.
