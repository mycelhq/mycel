# Editing and Post-Production

Editing is where footage becomes a video. The order of operations matters: lock story before you polish, because color and sound work on a cut that later changes is wasted labor. Never grade or mix a sequence whose structure isn't approved.

## Step 0: Ingest and organize

- Offload all media to two drives before touching it (3-2-1 backup: 3 copies, 2 media types, 1 offsite). Verify checksums on the offload.
- Build a project structure: bins for footage by scene, audio, music, graphics, exports. Rename or tag selects.
- Sync external audio to camera (by timecode or clap/waveform) and consolidate.
- **Make selects first.** Watch everything, mark the usable takes and best soundbites. For interview-driven pieces, transcribe and build the story from the transcript (a paper/text edit) before touching the timeline — this is how you find the narrative fastest.

## Step 1: Assembly, then rough cut

- Assembly: lay selects in order following the script/story spine. Ugly and long is fine.
- Rough cut: tighten to structure — hook in the first 3 seconds, clear arc, one CTA. Kill anything that doesn't earn its place. Ruthlessly cut duration; almost every first cut is too long.
- **Show the client the rough cut before polishing.** Structural changes are cheap now and expensive after color/sound. Frame the review: "This is picture structure and pacing — audio and color come after lock." This manages expectations and prevents them reacting to the rough grade.

## Step 2: Fine cut and pacing

- Cut on motion and on the beat. Match action across sizes. Vary shot length to control rhythm — shorter cuts accelerate energy, longer holds create weight.
- Use J and L cuts (audio leads or trails picture) to make dialogue and interview cuts feel seamless and hide jump cuts.
- Cover jump cuts in interviews with B-roll, not just because you can — motivate the cutaway by what the speaker is describing.
- Trim the "ums," dead air, and throat-clears, but leave enough breath that speech sounds human.
- Respect continuity and screen direction; don't cut two shots that break the 180.

## Step 3: Audio — the invisible half

Audio is ~50% of perceived quality. Do a real mix:
- Set a dialogue anchor level and ride it consistent (broadcast target around -16 LUFS integrated for web, ~-14 LUFS for many streaming platforms; confirm the platform spec). Web/social needs to be intelligible on phone speakers — check on the worst speaker you have.
- Clean dialogue: noise reduction for hiss/HVAC, de-ess, gentle EQ (roll off low rumble, presence lift), compression to even it out.
- Music: pick a licensed track that matches energy; duck it under dialogue (sidechain or manual automation) so speech always wins. Cut music to hit picture edits.
- Fill silence with room tone so cuts don't "drop out." Add subtle SFX/whooshes on graphic transitions where they earn it.

## Step 4: Color

- Correct first (balance, exposure, white balance shot-to-shot so the sequence is consistent), then grade (creative look).
- Match shots within a scene — nothing breaks immersion like skin tone shifting across a cut.
- Keep skin tones natural; watch them on scopes (vectorscope skin line). Respect broadcast-safe levels if it airs.
- Grade on a calibrated monitor, not a laptop panel. Don't let the look fight the brand palette.

## Step 5: Graphics, titles, and versions

- Lower thirds, titles, and end cards on-brand (correct fonts, colors, logo lockups). Keep on screen long enough to read twice.
- Design for **sound-off viewing** on social: burn in accurate captions/subtitles. A large share of feed views are muted — no captions means no message.
- Export platform-correct versions: aspect ratios and durations per placement from the brief (16:9, 9:16, 1:1). Reframe deliberately for vertical — don't just crop the 16:9 and lose the subject.

## Delivery and review discipline

- Send review links with visible timecode and frame-accurate comments (a proper review tool, not "around the 30-second mark").
- **Cap and batch revisions.** State the number of included rounds in the brief. Consolidate all notes per round; refuse the drip of one comment at a time. Distinguish "this is broken" (fix) from "I'd have done it differently" (discuss/scope).
- Final master: highest-quality mezzanine export archived, plus compressed platform deliverables. Deliver source project/archive if contracted.

## Quality bar

- **Great:** hooks in 3 seconds, no wasted second, dialogue crisp on phone speakers, color consistent shot-to-shot, captions accurate, correct versions per platform, passes in one or two review rounds.
- **Failure:** inconsistent audio levels, mismatched color across cuts, wrong aspect ratio for the placement, no captions on social, or endless revision rounds because story wasn't locked before polish.
