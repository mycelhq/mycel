# Policy Review and Claims Support

Placing a policy is not the end of the job. Clients' lives and businesses change, coverage silently drifts out of alignment with exposure, and the true test of the whole relationship comes at claim time. This skill covers two ongoing duties: the periodic review that keeps coverage right, and the claims advocacy that is the entire reason insurance exists.

## Periodic policy review

Review each client's coverage at least annually and after any major life or business change. The point is to catch the gap *before* the loss, not after.

- **Re-run the exposure check**: new home, renovation, new vehicle, marriage/divorce, new baby, business growth, new location, new product line, more employees, higher revenue, new contracts with insurance requirements, new cyber/data exposure. Each can create an uninsured gap or a limit that no longer fits.
- **Check for underinsurance and inflation**: rebuild costs and asset values rise; a limit set three years ago may now trigger a coinsurance penalty or leave the client unable to rebuild. Update property values to current replacement cost. Confirm liability/umbrella limits still match net worth.
- **Hunt for gaps and overlaps**: perils excluded and unaddressed (flood, earthquake, cyber, sewer backup, business interruption), lapsed or non-renewing coverage, and redundant coverage the client is double-paying for.
- **Re-market when warranted**: at renewal, verify the rate is still competitive and the carrier still strong; remarket if the renewal jumps or the carrier's rating slips — but weigh continuity and claims history against a small saving.
- **Document the review and any recommendations declined** — same E&O discipline as the needs analysis.

Deliver the client a plain-language summary: what changed, what's now exposed, what you recommend. Proactive reviews are what turn a transactional buyer into a lifetime client.

## Claims support — advocate for the client

When a loss happens, the client is stressed and often unfamiliar with the process. You represent *them*, not the carrier. Your advocacy determines whether they're made whole.

1. **Report promptly and correctly.** Most policies require timely notice; late reporting is a common denial reason. Get the claim filed with complete, accurate facts, the date/cause of loss, and supporting detail.
2. **Confirm coverage and set expectations.** Read the policy against the loss — what's covered, the applicable limit, the deductible, any sublimit, and the valuation basis (replacement cost vs actual cash value; the client may need to actually repair/replace to collect full RCV). Tell the client honestly what to expect, including their out-of-pocket.
3. **Help document the loss thoroughly.** Guide the client to photograph/video damage, keep damaged property until the adjuster releases it, itemize losses, gather receipts/valuations, and log additional living expenses or business-interruption losses. Strong documentation is the difference between a full and a partial payout. Advise them to mitigate further damage (a policy duty) and keep receipts for that too.
4. **Manage the adjuster relationship.** Coordinate the carrier's adjuster's inspection, be present or prepared, and make sure the adjuster sees the full extent of the damage. For large or complex losses, consider whether a public adjuster or contractor estimate is warranted to counter a low estimate.
5. **Review the settlement offer critically.** Check it against the documented loss and the policy — was everything included, the right valuation applied, depreciation correctly handled and recoverable? If it's low or a coverage position seems wrong, **push back with policy language and evidence**, and escalate or invoke appraisal/dispute provisions where justified. Don't let a client accept an unfair offer out of exhaustion.
6. **See it through** to payment and, where relevant, help ensure repairs happen and recoverable depreciation is collected. Then debrief: did the coverage hold up, and does the review need updating?

## Quality bar and failure modes

Great: annual reviews keep coverage matched to changing exposure with no silent gaps, and at claim time the client reports fast, documents thoroughly with your guidance, and collects a full, fair settlement because you advocated with policy language and evidence. The client feels represented, not abandoned. Acceptable: reviews happen, claims filed promptly, settlements checked. Failing: set-and-forget policies that drift into underinsurance or uncovered gaps, missing an inflation-driven coinsurance penalty, late or incomplete claim reporting, letting the client under-document a loss, taking the carrier's first offer at face value, or going passive during the claim — the moment the client most needs the broker.
