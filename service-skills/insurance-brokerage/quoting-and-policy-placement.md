# Quoting and Policy Placement

With the needs analysis done, you now shop the risk across carriers and place coverage. The amateur move is to sort quotes by premium and pick the cheapest. The professional move is to compare on *coverage delivered per dollar* — because the cheapest policy is usually cheapest for a reason (lower limits, worse exclusions, a shakier carrier), and that reason surfaces at the worst possible moment: a claim.

## Prepare a clean submission

Carriers price what they can see and load for what they can't. A complete, well-presented submission gets better terms. Assemble: accurate exposure data from the needs analysis, loss history / loss runs (typically 3–5 years — a clean record earns credits), property details (construction, age, roof, protective features, occupancy), operations description for commercial risks, and any risk-mitigation the client has in place (alarms, sprinklers, safety programs, backups). Present the risk honestly but in its best true light — highlight the positives, never misrepresent. A material misrepresentation can void the policy and expose you.

## Market to the right carriers

Match the risk to carriers with appetite for it — placing a risk with a carrier that doesn't want that class yields a bad price or a decline. Use appointed markets and, for hard-to-place or specialty risks, wholesale/excess-and-surplus (E&S) markets. Submit to enough carriers for genuine competition, and note which are admitted vs non-admitted (non-admitted/surplus lines aren't backed by the state guaranty fund — disclose this to the client). Check each quoting carrier's **financial strength rating** (e.g. AM Best) — an A-rated carrier that pays claims beats a cheap unrated one that might not be solvent when the client needs it.

## Compare on coverage, not price

Build an apples-to-apples comparison across quotes. Normalize and scrutinize:

- **Limits and sublimits** — are they actually equal? A lower premium often hides lower limits or a restrictive sublimit (e.g. on water damage, cyber, or specific perils).
- **Deductibles** — including special deductibles (wind/hail, named storm, percentage deductibles) that can dwarf the base.
- **Exclusions and endorsements** — the real differences live here. Read what each policy *doesn't* cover. One carrier's exclusion of a peril the client is exposed to can make a "cheaper" quote far worse.
- **Coverage form** — named-perils vs all-risk/open-perils; replacement cost vs actual cash value; occurrence vs claims-made (for liability/professional — claims-made needs tail/retro-date attention).
- **Conditions** — coinsurance, valuation, claim-reporting requirements, warranties.
- **Carrier quality and service** — financial rating and claims-paying reputation.

Present the client a clear side-by-side with your recommendation and the reasoning — not just numbers, but "this one costs a bit more but covers X which you're exposed to." Let them make an informed choice; document what they pick and why.

## Bind and place correctly

- **Verify the terms before binding** match what was quoted and what the client approved — carriers sometimes adjust terms or add conditions. Don't bind on assumptions.
- **Confirm effective dates and continuity** — no gap between an expiring policy and the new one (a lapse can be catastrophic and can raise future rates). Coordinate the effective time precisely.
- **Meet conditions and warranties** — if the quote is conditioned on an inspection, a protective safeguard, or a subjectivity (signed application, updated values), satisfy it before or promptly after binding, or coverage can be jeopardized.
- **Deliver and explain the policy**: issue proof of coverage (certificates/ID cards as needed), walk the client through what they bought — key limits, deductibles, exclusions, and how to file a claim — and confirm any additional insureds or lender/certificate requirements are handled.

## Quality bar and failure modes

Great: a complete honest submission marketed to in-appetite, financially strong carriers, a true coverage-based comparison that exposes the differences behind the premiums, a documented recommendation, and a clean bind with no coverage gap and all conditions met. The client understands exactly what they bought. Acceptable: competitive quotes obtained, compared on coverage, placed correctly with continuity. Failing: picking the cheapest quote without reading exclusions and sublimits, placing with an unrated or wrong-appetite carrier, misrepresenting the risk, binding on unverified terms, creating a coverage lapse, or ignoring a subjectivity/warranty that later voids a claim.
