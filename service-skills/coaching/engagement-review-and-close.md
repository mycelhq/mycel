# Engagement Review and Close

Most coaching ends by simply running out of sessions — a wasted opportunity that leaves value unproven and change unconsolidated. A professional engagement has deliberate review points that measure results against the baseline, transfer ownership to the client so gains persist, and make an intentional decision about what comes next. How you close determines whether the client sustains the change, renews, and refers.

## The mid-point review

Roughly halfway through, pause normal coaching for a dedicated review:
- **Re-measure against the intake baseline.** Re-run the self-assessments on the target dimensions; for executive engagements, pulse the same stakeholders you interviewed at intake ("what's changed in the last three months?"). This mid-course data tells you what's working and what isn't — while there's still time to adjust.
- **Review the commitments log** for patterns: consistent follow-through vs. persistent avoidance. The avoidance is the remaining work.
- **Recontract if needed.** Goals set at intake sometimes turn out to be the wrong goals once the real issue surfaced. If so, explicitly renegotiate the outcomes rather than quietly drifting. Confirm the client still owns the goals and the cadence still fits.
- **Recalibrate** frequency, commitment size, or focus based on the evidence.

## The closing review

The final session (or a dedicated closing session) is distinct from a normal session. Cover:
- **Measure final outcomes against baseline.** Self-ratings then vs. now, stakeholder input then vs. now, and any business metrics tied to the goals. Show the delta concretely. This is where you demonstrate ROI — for sponsored executive coaching, a theme-level progress summary (respecting the confidentiality contract) is what justifies renewal and referrals.
- **Have the client articulate their own learning.** Ask them to name what changed, what they learned about themselves, and what they're now able to do that they couldn't before. Their words, not yours — client-authored takeaways are what persist. Bank the wins explicitly; clients discount their own progress.
- **Consolidate the change into identity.** Move from "I did these things" to "I am now someone who…" — the shift from behavior to identity is what makes change durable after the coaching stops.

## Secure sustainability — the real test

The purpose of closing is a client who thrives without you. Before ending:
- **Build the maintenance plan.** What structures, habits, and environmental supports will keep the change alive? Which existing routines anchor the new behaviors? Who is their ongoing accountability now that you're not?
- **Anticipate relapse.** Name the situations most likely to trigger old patterns and rehearse the response. Change under stress is the true test; prepare for it explicitly.
- **Transfer the coaching skill.** By now the client should be able to run the GROW arc on themselves — set their own goals, question their own assumptions, hold their own accountability. Confirm they can. The best outcome is that they've internalized the coach.

## Decide the next step intentionally

Don't default. Choose deliberately among:
- **Clean close** — goals met, client self-sufficient. Celebrate the completion; a decisive, dignified ending is itself valuable.
- **New engagement** — real new goals have emerged (not manufactured to extend revenue). Recontract fresh, with a new baseline and outcomes. Be honest with yourself: extending a client who's done is a disservice and creates dependency.
- **Lighter maintenance cadence** — monthly or quarterly check-ins to protect the gains during the vulnerable post-engagement period.
Make the recommendation in the client's interest, not the revenue's — integrity here is what earns referrals and long-term reputation.

## Feedback and closeout logistics

- **Solicit honest feedback** on the engagement: what helped most, what you could have done better, what they wanted more of. Ask genuinely; it improves your practice and models the openness you coached.
- Handle final logistics cleanly: final payment, any deliverable summary, and (with permission) a testimonial or reference for sponsored engagements.
- Leave the door open warmly without pressuring a renewal.

## Quality bar

- **Great:** outcomes re-measured against baseline with a demonstrable delta, the client articulating their own learning and identity shift, a concrete maintenance-and-relapse plan, the coaching skill transferred so they're self-sufficient, an intentional (not defaulted) next-step decision made in the client's interest, and honest feedback gathered. The client leaves better and independent.
- **Failure:** the engagement just running out with no measurement, no proof of value, no maintenance plan, a client still dependent on you, or a renewal pushed for revenue rather than need. An unmeasured, unconsolidated ending squanders the entire engagement's value at the finish line.
