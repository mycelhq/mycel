# KPI Dashboard & Monthly Financial Review

The close produces statements; the review produces decisions. Your job is to distill the financials into the handful of metrics that actually drive this business, track them over time, and lead a monthly conversation that ends with the owner knowing what to do differently. A dashboard is not a data dump — it's an editorial act: choosing what matters and making the signal loud.

## Choose the right KPIs (metric selection is the skill)

Pick 8–12 metrics across four lenses, tailored to the business model. Vanity metrics (gross revenue alone, social followers) are out; decision-driving metrics are in.

- **Profitability:** gross margin %, net margin %, contribution margin by product/service line, EBITDA. Margin trend matters more than absolute profit.
- **Liquidity & solvency:** current ratio (current assets ÷ current liabilities; >1 healthy), quick ratio, cash runway (cash ÷ monthly burn), debt-to-equity, debt service coverage ratio (DSCR — key if there's a bank loan covenant).
- **Efficiency / working capital:** DSO (A/R days), DPO (A/P days), DIO (inventory days), cash conversion cycle, revenue per employee, utilization (for services), inventory turns.
- **Growth & pipeline:** revenue growth (MoM/YoY), new vs. recurring revenue, MRR/ARR and churn for subscription, backlog/pipeline coverage, CAC, LTV, LTV:CAC.

Match to model: a SaaS client lives on MRR/churn/CAC payback; a restaurant on food cost %, labor %, and covers; an agency on utilization and effective hourly rate; a contractor on backlog and job-level gross margin. Using generic metrics for a specific business is a tell that you don't understand it.

## Present for insight, not decoration

- **Trend over point-in-time:** every KPI shown as a trailing 12-month sparkline/trend, not just this month's number. Direction beats level.
- **Context:** actual vs. budget vs. prior year vs. target. A number without a comparison is meaningless.
- **Benchmarks:** compare margins/ratios to industry norms where available, and to the business's own history.
- **Hierarchy:** lead with the 2–3 metrics that are off-track this month. Don't make the owner hunt.
- Follow good data-viz discipline: clean, consistent, one story per chart.

## Run the monthly review (agenda)

1. **Headline:** how the month went in one sentence, and the single most important thing that changed.
2. **P&L walk:** revenue vs. plan, margin movement and *why* (mix, pricing, cost creep), notable expense variances.
3. **Cash & liquidity:** current balance, runway, receivables health, any covenant proximity.
4. **KPI scorecard:** what's green/red vs. target, and the trend.
5. **Diagnosis → decisions:** this is the point. For each red metric, name the likely cause and a recommended action with an owner and a deadline. "Gross margin fell 6 pts on the new supplier's pricing — recommend a 5% price increase on the affected line, effective next month; I'll model the elasticity."
6. **Follow-ups from last month:** did prior actions move the number? Close the loop.

## Quality bar

**Great:** a tight, model-appropriate KPI set with trends and comparatives, a review that always ends in 1–3 concrete decisions with owners and deadlines, causal explanations (not just "revenue was down"), and accountability for prior actions. The owner leaves knowing exactly what to change. **Acceptable:** a consistent dashboard and a walk-through of the statements with big variances flagged. **Failing:** a 40-metric dashboard with no hierarchy, metrics that don't fit the business, numbers with no comparison or trend, or a review that reports history without producing a single decision. If the meeting doesn't change what the owner does next month, it failed — the financials were the input, judgment is the deliverable.
