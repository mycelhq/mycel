# Pricing & Profitability Analysis

Pricing is the highest-leverage lever an SMB owner has — a 1% price increase typically drops far more to the bottom line than a 1% volume increase or cost cut, because it flows straight through with no added cost. Yet most SMBs price by gut or by matching competitors. Your job is to reveal what's actually profitable and set prices grounded in cost, value, and margin math.

## First, find true profitability (the diagnosis)

Owners routinely misjudge which products, services, and customers make money. Do the work:

- **Contribution margin per unit/line:** price − variable cost per unit. Then contribution margin ÷ price = contribution margin %. This, not gross revenue, tells you which lines earn.
- **Fully-loaded cost:** allocate not just direct materials/labor but the overhead each line actually consumes — support time, rework, shipping, payment fees, returns. Activity-based thinking exposes the "big" customer who's unprofitable after all the hand-holding.
- **Customer-level profitability:** rank customers by contribution after their true cost to serve. Expect a Pareto pattern — a minority drive most profit, and some large-revenue customers lose money. This drives fire/reprice/renegotiate decisions.
- **Product-line profitability:** identify the loss leaders vs. the margin engines. Sometimes the best move is to kill or reprice the low-margin line consuming capacity.

## Costing correctly

- Separate **variable** (scale with volume: materials, direct labor, commissions, payment fees, freight) from **fixed** (rent, salaries, software). Contribution margin uses variable cost; breakeven uses fixed.
- **Breakeven volume** = fixed costs ÷ contribution margin per unit. Every pricing scenario should show how breakeven moves.
- For services, compute the **effective hourly cost** including non-billable time, benefits, and overhead — the true floor beneath any rate.

## Pricing methods (pick deliberately)

1. **Cost-plus:** cost × (1 + target markup). Simple, guarantees margin, but ignores value and leaves money on the table. A floor, not a strategy.
2. **Market/competitive:** anchored to what rivals charge. Necessary awareness, but racing to match commodity pricing destroys margin — differentiate instead.
3. **Value-based (usually the goal):** price to the economic value delivered to the customer (time saved, revenue generated, risk avoided). Requires understanding the customer's ROI. Commands the highest sustainable margins. Move clients toward this wherever the value is quantifiable.

## Levers and tactics

- **Segmentation & tiering:** good/better/best captures both price-sensitive and premium buyers; the middle tier anchors. Add a premium tier even if few buy it — it lifts the reference price.
- **Value metric:** charge on the axis that scales with customer value (per seat, per usage, per outcome) rather than flat.
- **Discounting discipline:** every discount is pure margin given away — model the volume increase required to break even on a price cut (often implausibly large). Prefer added value over price cuts.
- **Raising prices:** most SMBs are underpriced. Model elasticity: even if you lose some volume, higher price × lower volume often beats the status quo. Grandfather existing customers or phase increases to manage churn. Test on new customers first.

## Presenting a pricing recommendation

Quantify the bottom-line impact of each scenario (price ±%, expected volume response, resulting contribution and net profit). Show the breakeven elasticity — "you'd have to lose more than 18% of volume for this 10% increase to lose money; that's unlikely given your differentiation." Give the owner a clear recommendation with the risk named.

## Quality bar

**Great:** true fully-loaded profitability by product and customer, contribution-margin and breakeven math, a value-based pricing recommendation with quantified P&L impact and elasticity analysis, and a specific action list (reprice these, fire/renegotiate those, add a premium tier). **Acceptable:** contribution-margin-by-line analysis and a defensible cost-plus/market recommendation. **Failing:** recommending prices off gross-revenue rankings (missing that big customers lose money), ignoring overhead in cost-to-serve, reflexive discounting to win volume, or a pricing suggestion with no modeled profit impact. The deliverable is money found — show the owner exactly how much and where.
