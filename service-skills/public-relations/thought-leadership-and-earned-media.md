# Thought Leadership and Earned Media

You are building a client (usually a founder or executive) into a recognized voice in their field, and converting that authority into earned media. This is a compounding, relationship-driven program — not a one-off placement. Authority comes from consistently saying something genuinely useful and being reliably available to the press.

## Step 1 — Define the platform (what they're known for)

Pick 2–3 narrow territories the client can credibly own — their real expertise intersected with what the market cares about and what competitors *aren't* saying. Vague ("leadership," "innovation") is invisible; specific and slightly contrarian is memorable ("why most B2B onboarding fails in week two"). Everything the client publishes and comments on should reinforce these territories so the authority accretes rather than scatters.

## Step 2 — The earned-media toolkit

- **Contributed articles / bylines:** pitch original, non-promotional expert pieces to trade and business publications (and their contributor programs). The article teaches or takes a stance; the credibility is the byline, not a product plug. Editors want insight their readers can't get elsewhere — a fresh framework, proprietary data, a strong opinion. One great byline in a respected trade outlet beats ten in content mills.
- **Expert commentary / source requests:** register and monitor journalist-request services (Qwoted, Featured/HARO-successor, SourceBottle, Help a B2B Writer) and reporter callouts on X/LinkedIn. Respond **fast** (often within an hour — first useful response wins), on-point, quotable, and within the journalist's exact ask. This is the highest-ROI earned-media tactic for a new voice.
- **Newsjacking / reactive commentary:** when news breaks in the client's domain, quickly offer the client as an expert take to relevant reporters, and publish a sharp reaction on the client's owned channels. Speed and a genuine angle are everything — the window is hours. Only comment where the client has real authority; forced hot-takes backfire.
- **Podcasts & speaking:** pitch the client to relevant podcasts and event/panel organizers with a specific, audience-relevant topic (not a bio). Podcasts are high-trust, long-form authority builders and easy to land relative to top-tier press.
- **Owned thought leadership:** a consistent LinkedIn/newsletter/blog presence in the client's voice is the base layer that makes them look credible when a journalist Googles them and gives reporters evidence of expertise. It compounds and feeds all the above.
- **Awards & rankings:** identify credible industry awards/lists, track deadlines, and write strong, evidence-backed submissions. Legitimate recognition is durable third-party proof.

## Step 3 — Package the client as a source

Maintain a ready **spokesperson kit:** bio (short/long), headshots, the 2–3 territories with sharp POVs, a bank of quotable talking points and stats, and instant availability. When a reporter needs a source now, the client must be reachable and pre-briefed. Media-train them: bridge to key messages, speak in clean quotable soundbites, stay in their lane, never speculate or go off-record naively.

## Step 4 — Operate the program

Run it as an always-on cadence, not campaigns: daily monitoring of source requests and breaking news, a steady byline pipeline (pitch ahead, one editor relationship at a time), consistent owned posting, and a running awards/speaking calendar. Nurture editor and producer relationships the same way as reporters — be reliable, useful, and never over-pitch. Track placements, the outlets' authority/reach, resulting inbound (leads, speaking invites, follow-on press), and search visibility for the client's name.

## Quality bar

- Every placement reinforces the defined 2–3 territories.
- Contributed content is genuinely useful/insightful and non-promotional.
- Source-request responses go out fast, on-topic, and quotable.
- Newsjacking is timely and within real expertise.
- The client is packaged and media-trained for instant availability.
- Program is consistent and compounding, measured on authority and inbound, not one-off hits.

## Failure modes

- Self-promotional "bylines" that read as ads → editors reject, readers ignore.
- Slow or off-topic responses to source requests → someone else gets quoted.
- Forced hot-takes outside the client's expertise → credibility damage.
- Scattershot topics that build no coherent authority.
- Treating it as episodic PR pushes instead of an always-on program.
- An unprepared, unavailable, or un-media-trained spokesperson missing every window.

**Great** looks like: a client known for a sharp, specific point of view, publishing useful bylines in respected outlets, quoted quickly and often as an expert, riding relevant news with real insight, present on the right podcasts and awards lists, and consistently visible on owned channels — an authority that compounds into durable, credible earned media and inbound opportunity.
