# Designing an outreach sequence

A sequence is the follow-up plan after the first message. Most replies do not come from touch one; they come from touch two or three, from people who saw the first, meant to reply, and forgot. The sequence exists to catch them without ever becoming pressure. The failure mode is the opposite: a rigid drip that keeps firing after the relationship has clearly gone cold, which is how an account gets reported.

## The rhythm

Three to four touches over two to three weeks is the sweet spot for LinkedIn. More than four unanswered messages reads as harassment and risks the account. The spacing widens as you go — a person who did not answer touch one in two days will not answer touch two in one; give them room.

A workable default cadence:
- **Touch 1 — the opener.** The connection note or first DM. (See the cold-opener skill.)
- **Touch 2 — a few working days later.** A short, genuinely useful follow-up: a relevant thought, a question, or a one-line resource named. NOT "just bumping this" and NOT "did you see my message." Add something.
- **Touch 3 — about a week after that.** The soft close: acknowledge they're busy, make it trivial to say no or not-now, leave the door open. "Totally understand if the timing's off — I'll leave it here. If [specific thing] ever becomes a priority, I'm around."
- **Touch 4 (optional) — a genuine break-up** weeks later, only if warranted. One line. Then stop.

## What each touch must respect

- **Every touch adds value or asks nothing.** A follow-up whose entire content is "following up" trains people to ignore you.
- **Read the room.** If they viewed your profile but didn't reply, that's interest — the next touch can be warmer. If there's zero signal after two touches, widen the gap or stop.
- **Stop on ANY reply.** The moment a human responds — even "not interested" — the sequence is over and a person takes it. A sequence that keeps firing after a reply is the single most damaging bug in outreach.
- **Weekday, business-hours, timezone-aware.** Sends land when the recipient is likely at their desk. Never a weekend blast, never 3am.
- **Pacing ceilings are sacred.** Volume per day per account stays under the platform's tolerance. A sequence that ignores pacing gets the whole account banned, which ends every relationship at once.

## Personalize the sequence to the lead, not just the opener

The touches should reflect what you know about this specific person: their role's real priorities, the industry's timing (end of quarter, hiring season), anything they've posted. A sequence designed for the ICP in the abstract is acceptable; one that reflects this lead's actual situation is what earns the reply.

## Great vs acceptable vs failing

- **Great:** 3–4 touches, widening spacing, each one adds something, tuned to the lead, stops instantly on reply, never crosses pacing limits. Reads like a thoughtful person following up.
- **Acceptable:** a sane cadence with generic-but-inoffensive follow-ups that stop on reply.
- **Failing:** more than four unanswered touches, "just bumping" filler, ignoring a reply, weekend/off-hours sends, or blowing past pacing. Any of these risks the account and the brand.
