# Visual Composition & Layout

This is the layer that separates "clearly made by a professional" from "clearly a template." The difference is almost never talent — it's discipline about a handful of variables. Apply them systematically; do not eyeball.

## Grid and alignment

Everything sits on a grid. Choose a column system (12-col is the workhorse) and a base spacing unit (8px, occasionally 4px for fine control). Every element aligns to the grid and every gap is a multiple of the base unit — no `13px`, no `27px`. Alignment is the cheapest way to look professional: pick edges (usually left-align text blocks) and hold them ruthlessly. Misalignment by a few pixels reads as "wrong" even to people who can't name why. Use optical alignment over mathematical where the eye demands it (e.g. centering a triangular play icon in a circle). Establish consistent margins and a max content width so text lines don't run too long.

## Spacing and the law of proximity

Whitespace is not empty — it's the primary tool for grouping and rhythm. Related items sit close; unrelated items get more space. The most common amateur mistake is *uniform* spacing everywhere, which destroys hierarchy: a heading should hug its paragraph and stand apart from the next section. Use a spacing scale with clear tiers (e.g. 4/8/16/24/48/96) and assign meaning — tight for intra-group, generous for inter-section. Premium design almost always uses *more* whitespace than feels comfortable at first. Cramped layouts read as cheap.

## Typographic craft

- **Scale**: use a modular scale (1.2–1.333 ratio); limit yourself to ~5 sizes. Random sizes = chaos.
- **Line length**: 45–75 characters for body. Wider is fatiguing; constrain with max-width.
- **Line height**: ~1.5 for body, tighter (1.1–1.3) for large headings. Never default single-spacing on body.
- **Contrast in weight/size** creates hierarchy — a bold 32px heading over a regular 16px body. Don't make everything medium-gray and medium-sized.
- **Tracking**: tighten large display type slightly, loosen all-caps/small labels. Never track body text.
- Limit to 2 type families. Establish and reuse the H1/H2/body/caption roles.

## Color and contrast

Follow a proportion discipline (60-30-10: dominant neutral, secondary, accent). Most of a good layout is neutral; color is used to *direct attention*, not to fill space. The accent should mark the primary action and little else — if everything is colorful, nothing stands out. Ensure text contrast meets WCAG AA (4.5:1 body). Beware low-contrast gray-on-gray "aesthetic" text that's actually unreadable. Use a consistent, considered neutral ramp rather than pure black on pure white (softens harshness) — but keep enough contrast.

## Hierarchy — guide the eye

Every composition needs a clear order: what the eye hits 1st, 2nd, 3rd. Build it with size, weight, color, and position/whitespace. Test it by squinting (or blurring the design) — the most important thing should still dominate. There is exactly one focal point per view. If two things fight for primacy, the design fails; demote one.

## Consistency and detail

Reuse the same components, spacings, radii, shadow styles, and icon weights everywhere — inconsistency is the tell of amateur work. Shadows should be soft and physically plausible (subtle, single light source), not harsh gray boxes. Border radii consistent across the system. Icons from one family at one stroke weight. Images treated consistently (same aspect ratios, same color treatment). Align imagery and text to shared edges.

## Avoiding the "AI slop" / template look

Reject: centered everything, generic drop shadows, purple-to-blue gradients on every card, evenly-spaced content with no hierarchy, stock-photo hero with text slapped on center, three identical feature cards with icon-title-paragraph, and rainbow color usage. Instead: asymmetry with intent, real hierarchy, generous and *varied* whitespace, restrained color, and one clear focal point per screen.

## Quality bar

Acceptable: aligned, readable, on-brand colors. **Great**: everything on a grid and spacing scale, a clear single focal point per view that survives the squint test, deliberate whitespace tiers creating rhythm, a tight type scale with real weight/size contrast, restrained accent color directing the eye, and pixel-consistent components/shadows/radii/icons throughout. Failure modes to reject: uniform spacing killing hierarchy, random font sizes, body lines running 120 characters, everything colorful, misaligned edges, and the tell-tale template symmetry. When in doubt: more whitespace, more contrast, fewer colors, one focal point.
