# Tax Planning & Quarterly Estimates

Tax prep is backward-looking; tax planning is where the money is saved. Your job is to project the client's liability during the year, keep them out of penalties, and execute deliberate strategies before year-end when they can still act. A return filed in April can only report decisions already made — planning changes the decisions.

## Quarterly estimated taxes

Self-employed and pass-through owners have no withholding, so they pay estimates. Compute required payments to avoid the underpayment penalty using the **safe harbor**: pay the lesser of 90% of the current-year liability or **100% of last year's tax (110% if prior-year AGI > $150k)**. Safe harbor is the floor — if income is way up, paying only safe harbor avoids penalty but leaves a big April bill; tell the client both numbers.

- Due dates: **April 15, June 15, September 15, January 15**. (Note the uneven quarters.)
- Recompute each quarter from actual YTD income, not a static January guess — SMB income is lumpy.
- Include **self-employment tax** (15.3%) in the projection, plus state estimates.
- W-2 clients under-withheld: fixing withholding via a new W-4 is better than estimates because withholding is treated as paid evenly across the year (cures earlier-quarter shortfalls).
- Use the **annualized income installment method** (Form 2210 Schedule AI) for seasonal businesses so a big Q4 doesn't create fake earlier-quarter penalties.

## Core planning levers for SMB owners

- **Entity structure:** model whether an S-election saves SE tax. Rough test: if net profit comfortably exceeds a reasonable salary, the distribution portion escapes the 15.3% SE/payroll tax — net of the added payroll-admin cost and reasonable-comp requirement. Quantify it; don't recommend an S-corp reflexively for a $40k side business (the overhead eats the benefit).
- **Retirement plans (biggest lever):** SEP-IRA (up to 25% of comp), Solo 401(k) (employee deferral + employer profit share — usually allows the largest contribution for a solo owner), defined-benefit plans for high, stable earners. These reduce taxable income dollar-for-dollar and can be funded up to the filing deadline (SEP) — a rare after-year-end move.
- **QBI optimization:** for pass-throughs near the income threshold, wages, retirement contributions, and timing can pull income under the limit to preserve the 20% deduction. Model it.
- **Accounting-method timing:** cash-basis clients can accelerate deductible expenses into December and defer income to January (or reverse in a rising-rate year). Prepay expenses within the 12-month rule.
- **Section 179 / bonus depreciation:** time equipment purchases to the year they do the most good — not automatically the current year.
- **HSA:** triple tax advantage; max it if on a qualifying HDHP.
- **PTET election:** state pass-through entity tax as a SALT-cap workaround — often four/five figures of federal savings.
- **Loss harvesting / gain timing** on investments; **QCDs** for charitable clients over 70½; **bunching** charitable and medical into alternating years to clear the standard deduction.

## The planning cadence

1. **Post-filing (spring):** debrief the just-filed return — what surprised us, what to fix.
2. **Mid-year (summer):** recompute the projection with 6 months of actuals; adjust estimates.
3. **Q4 (Oct–Nov) — the money meeting:** project full-year income, then decide and execute: retirement funding, equipment purchases, income/expense timing, Roth conversions, entity elections for next year. This is when strategies are still actionable.
4. **Year-end (December):** confirm moves are executed before Dec 31 (most deductions are done-by-year-end, not done-by-filing).

## Quality bar

**Great:** a rolling projection updated quarterly, estimates that keep the client penalty-free without over-parking cash, a documented Q4 strategy session with quantified savings for each recommended move, and coordination between the entity return and the owner's 1040. **Acceptable:** safe-harbor estimates computed, one year-end planning touch. **Failing:** letting a client get blindsided by a large April balance plus penalty, recommending an S-corp or a strategy without quantifying it, or "planning" that's just telling them what they already owe.

Always show the client the dollar impact of each recommendation and the deadline to act. A strategy they don't execute in time saves nothing.
