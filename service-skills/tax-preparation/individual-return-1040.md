# Individual Return (Form 1040)

You are preparing an individual federal income tax return. Accuracy and completeness are the mandate: every dollar of income the IRS already knows about must appear, every legitimate deduction and credit must be claimed, and the return must survive matching against third-party reporting.

## 1. Intake and organize

Build a document checklist from last year's return plus the client's organizer. Expect and reconcile:
- **Income:** W-2 (wages), 1099-NEC/1099-K (self-employment), 1099-INT/DIV/B (investment), 1099-R (retirement distributions), SSA-1099 (Social Security), 1099-G (unemployment/state refund), K-1 (partnership/S-corp/trust), W-2G (gambling).
- **Deductions/credits:** 1098 (mortgage interest), 1098-T (tuition), 1098-E (student loan interest), 5498 (IRA contributions), childcare receipts, charitable acknowledgments, medical, HSA (5498-SA/1099-SA), property tax bills, estimated-tax payment records.

The IRS receives copies of every 1099/W-2. Anything missing from your return that they hold triggers a CP2000 notice. Cross-check against the prior year: an interest account that reported last year but not this year is a likely missing document, not a closed account — ask.

## 2. Filing status and dependents

Choose the optimal filing status. Married couples: model both MFJ and MFS if there's reason (income-driven student loan plans, large medical, liability separation) — MFJ usually wins but not always. Verify dependent eligibility (qualifying child vs. qualifying relative tests: relationship, age, residency, support). Dependents drive the Child Tax Credit, EITC, and education credits.

## 3. Income assembly

Enter all income. For self-employment, prepare **Schedule C** (see the deductions skill), which flows to **Schedule SE** for self-employment tax (15.3% on net SE earnings, with the employer-half adjustment). Capital gains go on **Schedule D / Form 8949** — verify basis on 1099-B (broker basis is often missing for older lots; wash sales must be respected). Rental income on **Schedule E**. Watch for taxable state refunds (only if the client itemized and got a benefit last year).

## 4. Adjustments (above-the-line — always check these)

They reduce AGI regardless of itemizing: HSA contributions, deductible IRA, SE tax deduction (half), SE health insurance, SE retirement (SEP/Solo 401k), student loan interest, educator expenses. Missing above-the-line adjustments is a top preparer error.

## 5. Standard vs. itemized

Compute both. Take the larger. Itemized (**Schedule A**): SALT capped at $10,000, mortgage interest (acquisition-debt limits), charitable (cash and non-cash — Form 8283 if >$500 non-cash), medical (only the excess over 7.5% of AGI). With the high standard deduction most filers take standard — but always verify, especially homeowners in high-tax states and high-medical years. Consider bunching strategy for future years.

## 6. Credits (dollar-for-dollar — do not leave on the table)

Child Tax Credit and Credit for Other Dependents, Earned Income Tax Credit (run the eligibility — many eligible filers miss it; also a high-audit area, document qualifying-child residency), Child and Dependent Care Credit (Form 2441), education credits (American Opportunity vs. Lifetime Learning — Form 8863; AOTC is partially refundable and usually better for undergrads), Saver's Credit, residential energy credits (Form 5695), Premium Tax Credit reconciliation (Form 8962 — required whenever a 1095-A exists, or the return rejects).

## 7. Other must-checks

- **Estimated payments & withholding:** enter all quarterly estimates and withholding; assess underpayment penalty (Form 2210).
- **State return(s):** part-year/multi-state allocation, and reciprocity agreements.
- **AMT** (Form 6251) for high earners with large SALT/ISO exercises.
- **QBI deduction** (Form 8995/8995-A): 20% of qualified business income for Schedule C/K-1 pass-through income, subject to income thresholds and SSTB limits.
- **IRA/retirement:** excess contributions, backdoor Roth (Form 8606 basis tracking), RMDs for 1099-R.

## Review before filing

- Does every third-party document appear on the return? (Match to the IRS's view.)
- AGI, taxable income, and tax reconcile; effective rate is plausible.
- Refund/balance due matches expectation; large swings vs. prior year explained.
- Direct-deposit info and SSNs correct (a transposed SSN or routing number is the most common costly error).
- E-file diagnostics clear; 8962 present if 1095-A exists.

## Quality bar

**Great:** every document reconciled, all adjustments/credits captured, standard-vs-itemized and MFJ-vs-MFS optimized, QBI and PTC handled, prior-year comparison sanity-checked, and a client-facing summary explaining the outcome and any planning moves for next year. **Failing:** omitting a 1099 (CP2000 waiting to happen), missing the EITC or education credit a client qualified for, or fat-fingering direct-deposit details.
