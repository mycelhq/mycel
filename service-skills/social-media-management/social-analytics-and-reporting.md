# Social Analytics and Reporting

You are proving and improving the value of a client's social presence. The job is to connect activity to outcomes, learn what content works for *this* audience, and turn that into next month's plan — not to screenshot the follower count.

## Pick metrics that map to the objective

Tie every metric to why the client is on social:
- **Awareness/reach goal:** reach, impressions, non-follower reach %, follower growth rate, share of voice.
- **Engagement/community goal:** engagement rate (by reach, not by followers), saves + shares, comments, DM volume, response rate/time, sentiment.
- **Traffic/lead/sales goal:** link clicks, landing-page sessions from social (UTM-tagged), conversions, leads, revenue, CAC — the metrics that survive a CFO's scrutiny.

**Engagement rate by reach** (engagements ÷ reach) is the honest core metric — it's normalized and not inflated by an inactive follower base. Non-follower reach is the truest discovery signal.

## Instrument attribution

Social platforms overstate their own contribution and undercount cross-device/offline. UTM-tag every outbound link (`utm_source=instagram`, consistent naming) and read GA4 for actual sessions/conversions rather than trusting in-app "clicks." For lead/sales clients, close the loop to the CRM/billing so you can report real pipeline from social, not form-fills. Acknowledge that dark social (DMs, screenshots) drives more than trackable links show — don't over-claim precision.

## Analyze, don't just report

The value is in the *why*, not the numbers:
- **Top vs bottom performers:** identify the 3 best and worst posts of the period and the pattern behind them — format, hook, topic, length, time. Read video retention curves to see where viewers drop.
- **Format/pillar performance:** which pillars and formats over/underperform this account's baseline. Reallocate the calendar toward winners.
- **Timing & cadence:** does posting time/frequency correlate with reach here (use the account's own audience-active data, not generic "best time to post" myths).
- **Trend over time:** compare to prior period and to the trailing average, not one lucky viral spike. Flag whether growth is accelerating or plateauing.
- **Benchmarks:** compare engagement rate to industry norms and to named competitors where data is available, so "2% ER" has context.

## Build the report

1. **Executive summary:** performance vs goal, one line, with the single most important takeaway.
2. **Headline metrics** vs prior period and target (the objective-relevant ones up top; vanity metrics, if shown at all, are context, not headlines).
3. **What worked / what didn't:** the top and bottom posts with the *lesson* extracted.
4. **Audience insight:** growth rate, notable sentiment or demographic shifts, recurring questions/themes from community.
5. **Next month's plan:** concrete bets derived from the data — "double carousel educational posts (2× the ER of single images), test 3 new hooks, shift posting to 8am." A report without forward bets is incomplete.

Deliver a written narrative alongside any dashboard (Metricool, Sprout, native, or Looker Studio). Clients act on the story, not the pivot table. Keep metric definitions and time windows consistent across months.

## Quality bar

- Metrics map to the client's stated objective; engagement rate is by reach.
- Attribution is UTM-tagged and reconciled to GA4/CRM, with honest caveats about dark social.
- Analysis extracts patterns and lessons, not just totals.
- Every report ends with specific, data-driven next-period bets.
- Trends and benchmarks give context; no naked absolutes or cherry-picked spikes.

## Failure modes

- Vanity headlines (follower count, impressions, likes) with no tie to goals.
- Engagement rate by follower count (inflated/dishonest) instead of by reach.
- Trusting in-app click counts over UTM/GA4.
- Reporting numbers with no "why" and no next action.
- Celebrating a viral outlier as if it were the new baseline.
- Changing metric definitions between reports so trends become meaningless.

**Great** looks like: objective-aligned metrics, honest attribution, a clear read on which content wins for this specific audience and why, benchmarked against past performance and competitors, ending with a concrete, evidence-backed plan for what to make more of next month.
