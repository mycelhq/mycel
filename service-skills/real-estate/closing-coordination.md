# Closing Coordination

Once an offer is accepted, the sale enters escrow / under contract — a dense sequence of contingencies, inspections, financing steps, and legal documents, all on deadlines, involving a half-dozen parties who don't talk to each other. You are the hub. Deals die in this phase from missed dates, unmet conditions, and title surprises. Your job is a relentless timeline and proactive coordination so closing happens on schedule with no last-minute fire drills.

## Build the critical-path timeline immediately

The moment the contract is signed, extract every dated deadline into a transaction timeline and work backward from the closing date:

- **Earnest/escrow deposit** delivered by its deadline (protects the buyer's good-faith standing).
- **Inspection period** and the response/repair-negotiation deadline.
- **Appraisal** ordered and received.
- **Financing/loan contingency** — appraisal, underwriting, and clear-to-close.
- **Title search and title commitment** review.
- **Contingency removal dates** (inspection, financing, appraisal, sale-of-home) — the highest-stakes dates in the contract; missing one can forfeit earnest money or void the deal.
- **Final walkthrough** and **closing/funding/recording** date.

Every one of these is a date you own. Track them and start chasing each item well before its deadline, not on it.

## Drive each contingency

- **Inspection**: coordinate access and timing; when issues surface, help the client evaluate and negotiate repairs, credits, or price — and get the resolution in writing as an amendment before the response deadline lapses.
- **Appraisal**: order promptly (financing depends on it). If it comes in low, surface the options immediately — renegotiate, buyer covers the gap, or challenge with comps. A low appraisal left unaddressed stalls the loan.
- **Financing**: stay in close contact with the lender. Confirm the buyer is responsive to underwriting document requests — the deal's most common late-stage killer is a buyer slow to send paperwork or, worse, making a credit-changing move (new car loan, job change) mid-escrow. Warn buyers explicitly not to open new credit or change jobs before closing.
- **Title/escrow**: get the title search done early. Resolve clouds — liens, unpaid taxes, boundary/easement issues, unreleased mortgages, name discrepancies — as soon as they surface, because they take time to clear and are the classic cause of a delayed closing. Confirm the escrow holder has everything they need.

## Coordinate the parties

Keep buyer, seller, both agents, lender, title/escrow, attorneys (where applicable), and inspectors moving in lockstep. Chase the slow link before it becomes a delay. Communicate status to your client at each milestone so they're never surprised, and give them a clear "here's what you need to do and by when" for each of their action items (deposit, documents, insurance, utilities).

## Approaching the table

- **Final walkthrough** shortly before closing: confirm the property's condition matches the contract, agreed repairs are done, and nothing has changed. Address discrepancies before, not at, closing.
- **Review the closing/settlement statement** (e.g. the Closing Disclosure) against the contract — prorations, credits, fees, and the final figures. Catch errors before signing.
- **Wire-fraud protection**: warn the client, in writing and verbally, to independently verify wire instructions by calling the title company at a known number — never trust wiring instructions sent by email, which are a prime fraud target. This warning has saved life-savings; skipping it is negligent.
- Confirm the buyer has funds ready, homeowner's insurance bound, and required documents/ID; confirm the seller has payoff figures and keys ready.

## Close and confirm

Ensure signing happens, funds are received, and the deed **records** — the sale isn't truly done until recording and funding, not just signing. Confirm possession/key handoff per the contract. Then close out: deliver copies of executed documents to the client, release/return earnest money correctly, and follow up warmly (the relationship and referrals live here).

## Quality bar and failure modes

Great: a live timeline where every deadline is hit early, contingencies cleared without drama, title issues found and fixed weeks ahead, the walkthrough and settlement statement reviewed carefully, wire fraud actively prevented, and the deal funds and records on the scheduled date. Acceptable: deadlines tracked and met, parties coordinated, closing completes. Failing: missing a contingency-removal date, discovering a title cloud at the eleventh hour, a buyer's unreturned loan documents blowing the close, skipping the final walkthrough, letting a settlement-statement error through, or failing to warn about wire fraud.
