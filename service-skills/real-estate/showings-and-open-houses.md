# Showings and Open Houses

Showings are where interest becomes intent. Your job is to maximize qualified traffic, present the home at its best, capture and follow up every prospect, and protect the seller's property and safety throughout. Volume alone is vanity — a showing program is working when it produces qualified buyers who write offers.

## Scheduling and access

- **Make it easy to see.** The harder a home is to show, the fewer offers it gets. Offer broad, flexible windows and fast confirmation. Use a lockbox/showing service with logged access so you know exactly who entered and when.
- **Qualify showing requests** for private tours: confirm the buyer is working with an agent or is pre-approved. Don't hand lockbox access or unescorted entry to unverified strangers — this is both a deal-quality and a safety issue.
- **Prep the seller each time**: lights on, blinds open, temperature comfortable, pets secured or removed, valuables and medications locked away, and the seller *out of the house* during showings — buyers won't speak freely or linger with the owner present.
- **Sequence the day** with enough buffer that groups don't collide, and stage the entry route so the strongest first impression (curb, foyer, main living space) leads.

## Running a showing / open house

- **Set the scene**: fresh air, soft lighting, subtle pleasant scent (avoid overpowering fragrance), quiet music optional. Open the space up; a bright, warm home shows larger.
- **Guide, don't hover.** Highlight the two or three standout features and the recent upgrades, then give buyers room to explore and imagine. Answer questions honestly; if you don't know, say you'll find out rather than guessing (misstatements create liability).
- **Sign everyone in** at an open house — name, contact, agent status, and how they heard about it. This list is your follow-up pipeline and your security log. A "just looking" neighbor and a serious relocating buyer both belong in your CRM with the right tag.
- **Read buying signals**: measuring rooms, returning to a space, discussing furniture placement, asking about the neighborhood/schools/timeline, or calling a spouse. These signal intent — note them and prioritize follow-up.
- **Security**: keep buyers within sight in occupied homes, don't let groups split you across floors, and secure the property fully when leaving (all doors/windows locked, lockbox re-secured, lights per seller instruction). Never leave a home unlocked.

## Capture feedback and report to the seller

After each showing, request feedback from the buyer's agent: interest level, price perception, and objections. Aggregate it. Feedback is the market talking — if multiple buyers say "great house, priced high" or flag the same defect (dated kitchen, small yard, road noise), that's actionable intelligence, not noise. Deliver the seller a regular showing report: traffic count, feedback themes, competing listings' activity, and a recommendation (hold, adjust price, address a specific objection). Sellers tolerate a slow market far better when they see honest, consistent reporting.

## Follow up relentlessly

- Contact every serious prospect (and their agent) within a day: gauge interest, answer questions, and ask directly whether they're considering an offer and what's holding them back. Deals are lost to silence far more than to price.
- For a hot prospect, create urgency honestly — other showings scheduled, an offer expected — never fabricated. Manufactured urgency that's exposed destroys trust and can be an ethics violation.
- Keep the seller informed of every genuine interest and any incoming offer immediately; agents have a duty to present all offers promptly.

## Quality bar and failure modes

Great: the home is easy to see, shows immaculately every time, every visitor is captured and followed up within a day, feedback is synthesized into clear seller guidance, and qualified interest converts to offers. The seller feels informed and the property stays secure. Acceptable: showings scheduled and hosted, home presentable, feedback collected, prospects followed up. Failing: hard-to-show access that suppresses offers, showing an occupied cluttered home with the owner hovering, unverified strangers given access, valuables left out, no sign-in or follow-up, sitting on feedback, fabricated urgency, or leaving a home unsecured.
