# Chase sequence — invoice 1043, Vasquez Cabinetry

*(Reference document. Vasquez Cabinetry is invented; every name, figure and date below is made up to
show the standard. Nothing in it is true of anybody.)*

This is one invoice across three rungs, so the escalation is visible as a shape rather than as three
unrelated emails. Note what changes between them and what does not: the facts, the ask and the pay
link are in all three, and only the temperature moves.

---

## Rung 1 — day 4 overdue, `reminder`

**Subject:** Invoice 1043 — £2,840

> Hi Marie,
>
> Invoice 1043 for the September fit-out came due on the 30th and I don't think it's landed yet —
> £2,840, and you can pay it here: [pay invoice 1043].
>
> If it's already gone through in the last day or two, ignore me. If it's stuck somewhere on your
> side, tell me where and I'll work around it.
>
> Tom

**Why it is written this way.** It is four sentences, it names the invoice, the amount and the date,
and it offers an out that costs the client nothing to take. "I don't think it's landed yet" leaves
room for us to be wrong, which we sometimes are, and a client who has already paid does not feel
accused. The ask is one thing: pay, or tell me what's blocking it.

---

## Rung 2 — day 12 overdue, `firm_reminder`

**Subject:** Invoice 1043 — £2,840, now 12 days overdue

> Hi Marie,
>
> Following up on invoice 1043 — £2,840, due 30 September, so twelve days now. Pay link: [pay
> invoice 1043].
>
> If there's a date it can be paid by, let me know what it is and I'll leave it with you until then.
> If something about the invoice is wrong, tell me and I'll fix it today.
>
> Tom

**Why it is written this way.** The age is now stated as a number, which is the only escalation in
it. The tone has not hardened — it has become specific. It asks for a *date* rather than for
payment, because a client who cannot pay today can almost always name a day, and a named day
converts the invoice from a problem into a scheduled event. The offer to fix a wrong invoice is not
politeness: unpaid frequently means disputed and nobody said so.

---

## Rung 3 — day 26 overdue, `final_notice`

**Subject:** Invoice 1043 — £2,840, 26 days overdue

> Marie,
>
> Invoice 1043 is £2,840 and 26 days past its due date of 30 September. Our terms are 30 days net,
> with work paused on accounts more than 30 days overdue — which this reaches on Thursday.
>
> I would much rather not pause anything. Pay link is [pay invoice 1043]; if you need a payment
> plan, say so today and we will set one up.
>
> Tom

**Why it is written this way.** It is formal and it is still not a threat. The consequence named —
pausing work — is one the contract actually contains, which is the whole test: a consequence you
have not agreed is a bluff, and a client only has to call it once. It gives a specific date rather
than "shortly", offers a way out that is not payment in full, and it still signs off as a person.

---

## What happens on day 36

Nothing is sent. Three messages have gone out, the client has not answered, and a fourth email is
not a new argument. The invoice goes to the founder with everything above attached and one line:
*"Vasquez Cabinetry, £2,840, 36 days, chased three times, no reply since the 30th. Your call."*

That is the deliverable at that point. Deciding whether to phone, pause, discount or write off is
not a message — it is a judgement about a relationship, and the person who owns the relationship
makes it.
