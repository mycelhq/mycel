# Outreach playbook

> **Founder-gated.** This wedge is run by the founder and is never exposed to an end client.
> A prospect we are selling to must never see this wedge, its output, or its scoring of them.

## Voice

Direct, plain, human. A peer who does the work, reaching another owner — not a marketing agency,
not a tutoring-center script, not Mycel's leftover beachhead. Mirror the prospect's language.
Default to the language of their public site.

Short. A first touch is 3–5 sentences on email, 2–4 on LinkedIn. If it needs a scroll, it is too
long.

## Structure of a first touch

1. **One concrete, specific observation** about *their* business — something visible in the record
   (a page, an unanswered comment, a query they should win, a post). Generic flattery reads as a
   template and is worse than no personalization. The test: could this sentence be sent to a
   different company in the same city? If yes, rewrite it.
2. **The problem, in their words** — not ours. The report that never becomes a page. The close that
   slips. The inbox that sits.
3. **What we do, in one sentence**, grounded in `offer.md` and the project's knowledge.
4. **One small ask** — 15 minutes, or a free audit of a public URL, or a pilot week. One ask, not two.

Never open with "I hope this message finds you well." Never open with our own name.

## Hard rules

- **Never send.** Every task produces a draft. A human approves it. This is enforced by the
  kernel's approval gate, and it is also the thing we are selling, so we model it.
- **Only businesses, only published contact points.** We contact a business on a channel that
  business published for contact. No personal numbers, no scraped emails, no messaging
  individuals.
- **Identify honestly** on first contact — who we are and that we do this work. Never imply an
  existing relationship, a referral, or a prior conversation that did not happen.
- **Ground every claim** in `offer.md` and project knowledge. No invented statistics, customers, or
  results. Anything you cannot ground goes in `unverified_claims` so the reviewer sees it before
  approving.
- **Honor an opt-out immediately and permanently.** "No", "stop", "don't contact me", or silence
  after the cadence ends all mean stop. Set `mark_closed`.
- **If this business sells visibility work and the first step is email**, the email must contain
  one checkable artifact: a named page on their site, or a live audit URL. A first email without
  that is the same template we already refused on our own outbound.

## Cadence

At most **three touches**, then stop.

| Touch | When | Angle |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | day 0 | The observation + the ask |
| 2 | +4 days | A different angle — never "just bumping this to the top of your inbox" |
| 3 | +7 days | A clean close: one line, easy to say no to, door left open |

Reply of any kind ends the sequence — a human takes the thread from there.

## What kills a thread

- More than one ask in a message.
- Pitching before saying anything true about their business.
- Claiming to know their numbers.
- Following up with no new information.
- Any pressure. If the fit is not there, closing the thread is the right outcome.
- "I help [audience] achieve [outcome]." Instantly a script.
