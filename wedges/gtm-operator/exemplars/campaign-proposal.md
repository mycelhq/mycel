## Who we are writing to

**The segment:** UK companies, 50–500 staff, in regulated sectors (financial services, health,
insurance), who have posted a compliance or risk role in the last six months.

**Why that last clause does the work.** A company with an open compliance role is a company that has
admitted internally it has a gap. That is the difference between a list of companies who *could*
buy and companies who have already had the argument you would otherwise have to start.

## The finding: 47 companies dropped on one check

The largest single cut — 340 to 141 — is the hiring signal, and it is worth being explicit about
what that buys and what it costs.

**What it buys:** your last campaign to an unfiltered list got a 2.1% reply rate. The same sequence
sent to companies with an open compliance role got 9.4%. That is not a small edge, it is a different
business.

**What it costs:** you are ignoring 199 companies who might well need you and have not admitted it.
That is a real trade and it is the right one at this volume — you have capacity for 82 conversations
this quarter, not 340, so the question is which 82.

---

## The sequence

Three touches over eleven days. Each one is short, and each one earns the next.

**Touch 1 — day 0.** One paragraph. Names the open role we saw and asks one question about how they
are covering it in the meantime. No pitch, no attachment, no call-to-action beyond a reply.

> Saw you're hiring a Compliance Manager. Out of interest — who's covering the FCA reporting until
> they start? Most firms your size hand it to the finance lead and it quietly eats their December.
>
> Happy to share what we see other firms doing if useful.

**Touch 2 — day 4.** Only to non-openers, and it is a different subject line rather than a
"following up". A follow-up to an email nobody opened is the same email.

**Touch 3 — day 11.** The close, and it is a permission close: "should I stop emailing?" That
converts better than a final pitch and it cleans the list honestly.

**Then stop.** Three touches, then the company goes back to the pool for 90 days. A fourth touch is
where reply rate goes up slightly and reputation goes down permanently.
