## Why — selection, not absorption

This is the distinction the rest of the report hangs on.

**You are barely absorbed.** Asked cold — no browsing — assistants do not know Brightline Dental. Two
of four returned a different practice with a similar name. That is normal for a single-location
practice and it is not this quarter's problem to solve; absorption is slow to earn and slow to lose.

**You are increasingly selected.** With retrieval on, your implant-cost page was chosen as a source
in 3 of 4 implant queries. Perplexity quoted the price band verbatim:

> "Brightline Dental lists single implants at $2,400–$2,900 including the crown, with the consultation
> credited against treatment."

That sentence exists because the page states the number plainly in a table, in one place, with the
caveat attached. The old page said "prices from $2,400" in a hero image. No assistant could read it.

**Read this as:** healthy and improvable. A practice never absorbed but often selected is exactly
the profile that responds to source work — which is what you are paying for. Do not let anyone sell
you brand-awareness spend to fix a retrieval problem.

## This week's work

Three pieces, sized. Start with the small one — it is a day's work and it is where the loss above
came from.

### Small — add structured opening hours and an emergency path (2–3 hours)

Your contact page has hours as an image. Add them as text with `openingHoursSpecification` markup,
and one paragraph naming what to do out of hours and the number to call. This is the exact shape the
competitor used to take "emergency dentist near me" off you.

**Why it works:** emergency queries are answered from structured facts, not from prose. An assistant
that cannot parse your hours cannot recommend you to somebody in pain at 9pm.

### Medium — an aftercare page, written as answers (1–2 days)

Six tracked queries are about recovery — "how long does implant recovery take", "what can I eat after
an extraction", "is swelling normal after a filling". You rank for none and you have no page for any.
Your competitors' aftercare pages are thin, so this is uncontested ground.

Write it as a question-per-heading page, each answer complete in its first two sentences, with the
clinical caveat after rather than before. An assistant lifts the first complete answer it finds; a
page that opens with "every patient is different" gives it nothing to lift.

**Why it works:** this is the same mechanism the implant page proved on your own site three weeks
ago. We are repeating a move that has already worked here, not trying a new theory.

### Large — the local implant cost study (3–4 weeks)

Nobody in the region has published real price data. You have three years of your own anonymised case
records. A page reporting what implants actually cost locally — ranges, what drives the difference,
how many cases needed a graft — is original data, and original data is the only thing that earns
citations you do not have to keep re-earning.

**Why it works:** every recommendation above makes an existing answer choose you. This one makes you
the source the answer is built from, which competitors cannot copy by writing a better page.

**Be honest about the cost:** this is weeks, it needs your clinical sign-off, and it will not move
next week's number. Do it because the first two will plateau in about a quarter.
