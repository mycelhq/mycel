# What actually gets a company cited

Working notes. Corrected as we learn — this is a young discipline and confident advice about it is
usually wrong.

## Holds up so far

**Specific, checkable claims get quoted. Superlatives get dropped.** "SOC2 Type II since 2024, 190
countries, average onboarding 4 days" survives into an answer. "The leading platform" does not — it
carries no information a model can attach to a question.

**Being the clearest source on a narrow question beats being the biggest name on a broad one.** A
company nobody has heard of wins "best payroll for UK startups hiring EU contractors" by being the
only page that answers exactly that.

**Comparison pages work, and they cut both ways.** Models lean on them heavily. A competitor's
"X vs Y" page is often *why* the model frames the comparison as it does — which means the remedy for
`competitor_favoured` is usually to write the honest version, not to complain.

**Structure helps.** Answers, headings and lists that map to how the question is asked get lifted
more or less whole.

## Doesn't hold up

**Keyword density.** Irrelevant. This isn't ranking.

**Volume.** Forty thin pages perform worse than four good ones, and risk the site being treated as
low quality.

**Chasing every model.** They disagree, and they change weekly. Track the movement across all of
them, not the position in any one.

## The uncomfortable bit

Some of this will be wrong within a year. Providers change retrieval and grounding without notice,
and a technique that works today can stop working in a week with no announcement. Which is the actual
argument for the service: measurement that runs continuously beats advice that was true once.
