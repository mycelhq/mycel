# A LinkedIn post — Fold & Crumb, brand studio

*(Reference document. Fold & Crumb and Hart's Bakery are invented; every figure, name and result
below is made up to show the standard. Nothing in it is true of anybody, and nothing in it may be
reused as though it were.)*

---

## Client summary

This is the mobile-orders story from the Hart's Bakery job, written for LinkedIn. It leads with the
number because the number is the reason anyone stops scrolling, and it spends most of its length on
the *one change* that caused it rather than on the redesign — which is what makes it useful to a
reader who is not going to hire anybody this week, and therefore what makes it get shared.

It names Hart's because you confirmed we may. It does not mention what you charge, and it does not
end with a call to action: your headline says what you do, and a post that asks for the work
performs worse than one that demonstrates it.

Expect it to do very little on the day and to be the thing a prospect mentions six weeks later.

---

## The post

> A bakery we rebuilt last month was losing about 80% of its online orders on mobile.
>
> Not to a competitor. To a button.
>
> Their order page looked fine on a laptop — the menu, the photography, the whole thing. On a phone
> the "Order now" button sat below three product shots and a paragraph about their sourdough starter.
> You had to scroll past all of it to buy anything, and 4 out of 5 people didn't.
>
> We moved the button above the fold on mobile and left almost everything else alone.
>
> Online orders went from 12 a week to 60.
>
> The design work mattered — they needed the photography and they needed the story. But the thing
> that moved the number was noticing that most of their customers were standing at a bus stop, not
> sitting at a desk, and building the page for the bus stop.
>
> If you sell anything online and you have never opened your own checkout on a phone, do that today.
> It is free and it takes ninety seconds.

## Why this and not the obvious version

The obvious version of this post is about the redesign — before and after, the photography, the
craft. It would be prettier and it would do nothing, because every studio posts that and a reader
cannot tell any of them apart.

This one works because it gives away the useful part. The reader can act on it without hiring you,
which is exactly why they remember who told them.

**What was deliberately left out:** the price, the timeline, the tech, and any sentence beginning
"in today's digital landscape". None of them help, and the last one is how a reader decides a post
was written by software.
