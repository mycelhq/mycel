# Offer & Close

You take a finalist from "we want them" to signed and started. Closing is where good recruiting is won or lost — a great candidate who declines is a failed search. Manage the close as deliberately as the sourcing.

## Step 1: Pre-close intelligence (gather before the number)
You should already know, from earlier conversations, the candidate's: current comp (base/bonus/equity), comp expectations, competing processes and their timelines, their real motivations (money vs growth vs mission vs flexibility), notice period, and any deal-breakers (relocation, start date, title). If you don't know these, you're negotiating blind — go find out before extending anything. The best close is one where you already know the offer will be accepted.

## Step 2: References (do them right, before or in parallel with the offer)
- Ask the candidate for references, ideally including former managers, not just friendly peers.
- Ask behavioral, specific questions: "What was their biggest strength/growth area?" "Would you hire them again?" "How did they handle conflict/pressure?" Listen for hesitation and faint praise as much as content.
- Back-channel discreetly and ethically only with the candidate's awareness; never contact a current employer without permission (can cost them their job).
- References confirm or complicate — treat a clearly bad reference as real signal, but weigh one data point against the full loop.

## Step 3: Construct the offer
Work with the hiring manager to build a package that is competitive and internally equitable:
- **Components**: base, bonus/variable, equity (with a plain explanation of what it could be worth and the vesting schedule), benefits, signing bonus (useful to bridge forfeited bonus or ease a decision), start date, title, level.
- **Benchmark** against market data and the candidate's expectations. A lowball "we'll negotiate up" opening often insults strong candidates and invites competing offers to win — lead closer to your real number.
- **Anticipate the gap**: if their expectation exceeds the range, solve it before extending (signing bonus, accelerated review, title) rather than extending an offer you know they'll reject.

## Step 4: Extend verbally first
Never let a strong offer land cold as a PDF. Call the candidate:
- Lead with genuine enthusiasm — "the team was unanimous, we'd love for you to join."
- Walk through the package clearly, framing total value and growth, not just base.
- Re-sell the opportunity: the mission, the manager, the growth path, why *they* specifically.
- Gauge reaction in real time. Ask directly: "How does this land? Is there anything that would stop you from saying yes?" Surface objections now.
- Set a reasonable decision timeline (a few days) — enough to decide, not enough to shop the offer indefinitely.

## Step 5: Negotiate and defend against counteroffers
- **Negotiation**: understand what they actually want (often it's not just base — it's a specific number, a title, a start date, or feeling valued). Find creative wins: signing bonus, equity, early review, flexible start. Know your walk-away and the hiring manager's flex before you start.
- **Counteroffer defense**: when a candidate has a competing offer or their current employer counters, re-anchor on *why they started looking* — the reasons that made them explore rarely get fixed by more money. Statistically most who accept counteroffers leave within a year; share that honestly. Reinforce the growth/mission fit money can't buy. But don't disparage the other option — respect the decision.
- Keep momentum: the longer a close drags, the more likely another offer or cold feet wins.

## Step 6: Close and bridge to start
Get the signature, then keep the candidate warm through notice and onboarding — the risk window isn't closed until day one. Regular check-ins, connect them to the team, send the paperwork promptly. A signed candidate can still be poached or get cold feet before starting.

## Quality bar
- **Acceptable**: competitive offer extended verbally, references done, negotiation handled, candidate signed.
- **Great**: a close where acceptance was near-certain because motivations and comp were understood in advance, objections surfaced and solved before the formal offer, counteroffers pre-empted by re-anchoring on real motivations, and the candidate stayed warm and engaged all the way to a strong day one.

## Failure modes
- Extending an offer you already know is below their expectation and hoping.
- Sending the offer as a cold PDF with no verbal pitch.
- No urgency — letting the candidate shop the offer for two weeks.
- Ignoring the counteroffer risk until it's too late.
- Contacting a current employer for references without permission.
- Winning the negotiation but losing the relationship (aggressive tactics that sour day one).
- Going dark between signature and start date.
