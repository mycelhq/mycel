# Screening Candidates

You filter sourced/applied candidates into a shortlist worth the hiring manager's time. The goal is a calibrated slate where every person is genuinely worth an onsite — false positives waste everyone's time, false negatives lose great people.

## Step 1: Resume/profile review — read for signal
Resumes are noisy. Read for evidence, not keywords:
- **Impact over responsibilities**: "led migration that cut latency 40%" beats "responsible for backend." Weight quantified outcomes.
- **Trajectory**: increasing scope/ownership over time. Lateral-only moves at senior level are a yellow flag worth probing, not rejecting.
- **Tenure patterns**: one short stint is nothing; a pattern of <1 year across many roles is worth a direct question. Don't auto-reject — the pandemic, layoffs, and startups create legitimate churn.
- **Relevance**: does the actual work map to the must-haves from the role brief? Adjacent experience with clear transferability counts.
- **Red flags to probe (not auto-reject)**: unexplained gaps, title inflation, vague team-vs-individual contribution ("we built"), buzzword salad with no specifics.
Score against the role brief's must-haves/nice-to-haves, not against a mental "ideal." Keep notes on why in/out — you'll need them for calibration and fairness.

## Step 2: Fairness & legal hygiene
- Evaluate consistently: same criteria for every candidate. Structured beats gut.
- Never screen on or ask about protected characteristics: age, race, religion, national origin, disability, pregnancy, marital/family status, etc. Don't infer from graduation years, names, or photos.
- Focus every question on ability to do the job. If you wouldn't ask every candidate, don't ask.
- Keep records of decisions and reasons (defensible, consistent, auditable).

## Step 3: The structured phone screen (20-30 min)
Use the SAME core questions for every candidate for the role — structured interviews are ~2x more predictive than unstructured chats. Structure:
1. **Frame (2 min)**: role, company, what the call covers, timeline.
2. **Motivation (5 min)**: why are they open to moving? What are they looking for? This surfaces fit and flight risk early. Listen for whether the role actually gives them what they want — a mismatch here means they'll churn or decline the offer later.
3. **Experience deep-dive (10 min)**: pick their most relevant project and go deep with behavioral, evidence-seeking questions. "Walk me through a hard problem you owned end to end." Probe for *their* specific contribution, decisions, and tradeoffs. Follow up on vague answers ("what exactly did you do?").
4. **Must-have verification (5 min)**: confirm the non-negotiables — key skills, work authorization, location/remote fit, comp expectations (get the number band early to avoid wasting everyone's time), availability/notice period.
5. **Their questions + sell (5 min)**: answer honestly, and if they're strong, actively sell the opportunity.

## Step 4: Score and decide
Rate against a simple rubric per competency (e.g., 1-4: no evidence / some / solid / exceptional). Require evidence for each score — "seemed sharp" is not a data point. Make a clear recommendation: advance / hold / decline, with the two or three reasons. Calibrate the first few screens with the hiring manager to align on the bar.

## Step 5: Candidate experience
Every candidate is a potential future hire, customer, or referrer. Respond promptly, be transparent about process and timeline, and always close the loop — even rejections deserve a prompt, respectful note. Ghosting damages the client's brand.

## Quality bar
- **Acceptable**: a shortlist scored against the role brief with structured notes, comp and logistics verified, candidates kept informed.
- **Great**: a tight slate where the hiring manager advances most of them, each backed by specific evidence, flight risks and comp mismatches surfaced *before* the onsite, and a documented, consistent, legally clean process.

## Failure modes
- Unstructured "vibe" screens that don't predict performance and invite bias.
- Not verifying comp/location/authorization early, then losing candidates deep in the process.
- Keyword-matching instead of reading for impact.
- Being lenient because the pipeline is thin — advancing weak candidates wastes the hiring manager's trust.
- Slow responses and ghosting that burn the client's employer brand.
- Asking anything touching protected characteristics.
