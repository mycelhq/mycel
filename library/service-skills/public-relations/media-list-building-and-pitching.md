# Media List Building and Pitching

You are earning a client media coverage. Coverage comes from giving a specific journalist a story their audience wants, delivered the way they work. Spray-and-pray blasts are the failure state and torch the client's reputation with the press.

## Step 1 — Define the story before touching a list

Journalists cover stories, not companies. Sharpen the angle first: what's *new, true, and relevant now*? Test it against news value — timeliness (news hook / hard peg), impact/scale, novelty, conflict/tension, human interest, or data/exclusivity. If the "story" is "our client exists and is great," you have no story — find the hook (a launch, a funding round, proprietary data, a trend the client can comment on, a contrarian take, a customer transformation). A newsworthy angle with a real peg is 80% of the win.

## Step 2 — Build a targeted list (small and precise beats large)

Research the actual reporters who cover this beat and audience. For each, confirm: they cover *this specific topic* (read their last 5–10 articles, not just their title), their outlet reaches the client's target audience, they're currently active (bylines in the last few months), and their preferred contact + pitch preferences (many state them on X/bio/outlet page). Sources: Muck Rack, Cision, Prowly, Roxhill, plus manual research — Google News, the outlet's beat pages, X, and Substack. Note each journalist's angle and recent work so you can personalize.

A tight list of 15–40 genuinely-relevant journalists outperforms a 500-contact blast every time. Segment by tier (tier-1 targets for exclusives, broader tier for the general send) and by beat.

## Step 3 — Write the pitch

Format: a short, personalized email — not a press release pasted into the body.
- **Subject line:** the story in ~6–10 words, specific and intriguing, no "PRESS RELEASE:" prefix. This decides whether it's opened.
- **First line:** show you know their work — reference a relevant recent piece *genuinely* (not "I loved your article"). Then the hook.
- **The story in 2–3 sentences:** why it matters to *their readers* now, the news peg, and what's new/exclusive. Lead with the news, not the company boilerplate.
- **Why them:** one line connecting the story to their beat.
- **The offer:** what you can provide — interview, data, exclusive, assets, expert source, embargoed access.
- **Make it easy:** key facts, a link to a press kit/assets, and your availability. Keep the whole thing under ~200 words. Journalists skim on mobile.
- Offer **exclusives or embargoes** to tier-1 targets — a reporter is far more likely to run a story they own. Honor embargoes absolutely.

## Step 4 — Timing and follow-up

Send early in the day, early in the week; avoid Fridays and major news days that will bury you. **Personalize each send** — mail-merge tokens are obvious and get deleted. One polite follow-up after 3–5 days if no reply, adding a new angle or asset, then stop. Never spam or guilt-trip. Track opens/replies/coverage in the CRM.

## Relationship over transaction

The best PR is a standing relationship. Be a reliable, fast, honest source; respect deadlines; never pitch a bad-fit story to a good contact. One burned journalist costs the client a channel for years. Feed reporters good stories even when it's not "your" news, and they'll take your calls.

## Quality bar

- Every pitch has a genuine news hook and a target-relevant angle.
- The list is hand-verified for beat fit and recency; no scraped mega-lists.
- Each pitch is personalized with real evidence of reading the reporter's work.
- Subject line sells the story in a glance; body is <200 words and leads with news.
- Exclusives/embargoes offered strategically and honored.
- Follow-up is one polite, additive touch — not nagging.

## Failure modes

- Blasting a generic press release to a bought list → spam-filed, reputation damage.
- Pitching reporters who don't cover the beat (title-matching instead of reading their work).
- Company-centric pitches with no reader-relevant hook.
- Fake flattery or wrong-article references — instantly transparent.
- Subject lines that bury the story or scream "PRESS RELEASE."
- Breaking an embargo or over-following-up.

**Great** looks like: a sharp, well-pegged story matched to a hand-built list of the right reporters, each pitched a personalized <200-word note that leads with why their readers care, tier-1 targets offered an exclusive — landing coverage and building relationships that pay off on the next story.
