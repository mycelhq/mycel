# Needs Analysis and Risk Assessment

Before you quote anything, you must understand what the client actually has to lose and how badly. A good broker's core value is not finding a cheap policy — it's identifying the exposures the client doesn't know they have and making sure a foreseeable loss doesn't ruin them. Skipping or rushing this phase is how clients end up underinsured, discover a coverage gap at claim time, and lose everything. Quote to the exposure, never the other way around.

## Discover the whole picture

Interview and fact-find systematically. For a **personal** client: assets (home value and rebuild cost, other properties, vehicles, valuables, watercraft), liabilities and net worth (net worth drives liability limits — a wealthy client with a minimum umbrella is a lawsuit away from ruin), household composition, drivers, occupation, income to protect, health and dependents, and risk tolerance. For a **commercial** client: the operations and industry, revenue and payroll, physical locations and property values, equipment, vehicles/fleet, employee count, products or services and their liability profile, professional exposures, contracts (what indemnities and insurance requirements they've signed), data/cyber footprint, and business-continuity dependencies.

Ask what keeps them up at night, and ask what would happen financially if each major asset were destroyed or they were sued. The goal is to see exposures the client can't articulate.

## Identify and prioritize exposures

Map exposures across the standard categories so nothing is missed: **property** (fire, weather, theft, equipment breakdown), **liability** (premises, auto, products, professional/E&O, employment practices, directors & officers), **business interruption / loss of income**, **workers' compensation** (statutory), **cyber/data**, **life/disability/income protection**, and specialty (flood, earthquake, marine, umbrella/excess). Note that flood and earthquake are typically **excluded** from standard property policies and need separate coverage — a classic, catastrophic gap.

For each exposure, assess **frequency** (how likely) and **severity** (how bad). Prioritize the high-severity risks even when low-frequency — the low-probability, business-ending loss (a liability judgment, a total fire, a flood) is exactly what insurance exists for. Don't let a client spend on cheap high-frequency/low-severity coverage while leaving a catastrophic exposure bare.

## Quantify the right amounts

- **Property**: insure to **replacement cost / rebuild cost**, not market value or purchase price — and watch **coinsurance** clauses that penalize underinsurance at claim time. Update values for inflation and improvements.
- **Liability**: set limits against net worth and exposure, and recommend an **umbrella/excess** policy where the underlying limits don't cover the downside. Umbrellas are cheap relative to the protection.
- **Business interruption**: calculate on actual lost income + continuing expenses over a realistic restoration period, not a guess.
- **Life/disability**: quantify income replacement and obligations (debts, dependents, education).

Match deductibles to the client's cash tolerance — higher deductibles lower premium but must be affordable when a loss hits.

## Document and set up for quoting

Record the analysis: exposures found, coverages recommended with limits and reasoning, gaps in current coverage, and anything the client **declined** (get declinations in writing — this protects the client's understanding and your E&O). This documented needs analysis becomes the spec you quote against and the record that you advised properly.

## Quality bar and failure modes

Great: a complete exposure map across all categories, high-severity risks prioritized, coverages quantified to replacement/rebuild cost and net worth, catastrophic gaps (flood, earthquake, umbrella, business interruption, cyber) surfaced, and every recommendation and declination documented. The client understands what they're protected against and what they chose to skip. Acceptable: main exposures identified and reasonably quantified, obvious gaps flagged. Failing: quoting before understanding the client, insuring property to market instead of rebuild cost, ignoring coinsurance, missing flood/earthquake/umbrella exposures, setting liability limits below net worth, or failing to document declined coverage — the setups for an uncovered claim and an E&O complaint.
