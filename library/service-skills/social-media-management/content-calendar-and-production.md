# Content Calendar and Production

You are running a client's organic social output. The goal is a consistent, on-strategy stream of content that ladders to a business objective — not "post something daily." Reactive, day-of posting is the failure state; you build a system.

## Step 1 — Anchor to strategy before scheduling anything

Confirm the client's objective (awareness, community, leads, sales, retention), target audience, and brand voice. Derive **content pillars** — 3–5 recurring themes the brand has authority to own (e.g., for a fitness studio: technique education, member stories, behind-the-scenes, myth-busting, offers). Every post maps to a pillar. Pillars keep the feed coherent and stop the "what do we post today" panic.

## Step 2 — Set cadence by capacity, not aspiration

Pick a frequency the client can sustain at quality. Better to post 3×/week consistently than 7×/week that collapses in a month. Platform realities:
- **Instagram:** 3–5 feed/Reels per week + daily-ish Stories; Reels carry reach.
- **TikTok:** volume rewards — often daily; native, fast.
- **LinkedIn:** 2–5×/week, weekday mornings, text/document/native video.
- **X:** higher frequency, real-time.
- **YouTube:** weekly long-form + Shorts.
Match format mix to each platform; never cross-post identically (a raw TikTok watermark on Reels gets suppressed; a LinkedIn essay dies on X).

## Step 3 — Apply a content-mix ratio

Balance value against promotion so the feed doesn't read as an ad. A workable default is ~80/20 (value/entertain/educate vs direct promotion), or the classic thirds: original value, curated/community, promotional. Weight toward the formats the platform's algorithm currently favors (short-form video across most platforms in this era).

## Step 4 — Build the calendar

Maintain a calendar (Notion/Airtable/Sheets, or the scheduler's planner) with, per post: date/time, platform(s), pillar, format, hook/caption, visual asset link, hashtags, CTA, status (idea → drafting → design → approval → scheduled → published), and owner. Plan a month ahead at the theme level, 1–2 weeks locked in detail. Seed the calendar from: evergreen pillar content, upcoming launches/promotions, seasonal/holiday moments (planned weeks ahead), and reserved slots for reactive/trend content.

## Step 5 — Batch production

Produce in batches by type, not one post at a time — batching is 3–5× more efficient. Script/outline a week of hooks, shoot video in one session, write captions in one session, design graphics in one session. Every asset passes a QA checklist before scheduling: correct aspect ratio per platform (9:16 vertical for Reels/TikTok/Shorts, 1:1 or 4:5 feed, 16:9 YouTube), captions/subtitles burned in for sound-off, safe zones clear of platform UI, links/handles correct, CTA present, brand fonts/colors, spelling.

## Step 6 — Schedule and hold reactive capacity

Schedule the batch via the client's tool (Later, Buffer, Sprout, Metricool, Hootsuite, or native Business Suite/Creator Studio). Prefer native publishing for Reels/TikTok when the algorithm penalizes third-party pushes. Leave ~20% of slots open for trends, news, and community moments — a fully pre-scheduled feed can't ride a trending sound or respond to the moment.

## Approval workflow

Route drafts to the client with enough lead time (a week for scheduled content) via a review link/board. Track approval status explicitly; never publish unapproved brand content. Keep a short "pre-approved evergreen" bank for gaps.

## Quality bar

- Every post maps to a pillar and a CTA/objective.
- Format and copy are platform-native, not copy-pasted.
- A month is visible at the theme level; the next 1–2 weeks are produced and approved.
- Assets pass the technical QA checklist (ratio, captions, safe zones).
- ~80/20 value-to-promo balance maintained.
- Reactive capacity reserved.

## Failure modes

- Posting reactively day-of → inconsistent quality and cadence collapse.
- Identical cross-posting → algorithmic suppression and off-voice content.
- Over-promotion → audience tunes out.
- Over-scheduling with zero room for trends/community → feed feels canned.
- No approval trail → brand-risk posts go live.
- Ignoring aspect ratio / sound-off captions → amateur-looking, low-reach posts.

**Great** looks like: a pillar-anchored, platform-native calendar produced in batches, approved ahead of time, technically clean, balanced value-to-promo, with headroom to jump on the moment — running like a newsroom, not a fire drill.
