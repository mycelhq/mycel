# Community Management and Engagement

You are the client's voice in every reply, DM, and mention. Publishing is half the job; the relationship happens in the interactions. Great community management turns followers into advocates and defuses problems before they escalate.

## Operating model

- **Triage inbound continuously,** not once a day. Categories: genuine questions (answer/route), sales intent (nurture → hand to sales/CRM), praise (amplify), complaints (resolve), trolls/spam (manage per policy), and crisis signals (escalate). Use a unified inbox (Sprout, Sparkle/Sprinklr, Agorapulse, or native Business Suite) so nothing is missed across platforms.
- **Response-time targets:** public comments and DMs within a few hours during business hours; complaints and anything with reach faster. Set expectations with an auto-reply for off-hours, but never let automation replace real replies. Speed is itself a loyalty and algorithm signal.

## Voice and reply craft

- Reply in the brand's defined voice — consistent tone, not a different personality per message. Keep a voice cheat-sheet (do/don't words, emoji usage, formality) and follow it.
- Be human and specific: use the person's name, reference their actual point, add value beyond "Thanks! 😊". Generic canned replies read as a bot and erode trust.
- Match energy: playful with playful, warm and efficient with a question, calm and accountable with a complaint.
- Encourage the next interaction: ask a follow-up, invite them to DM, point to a resource. Every reply is a chance to deepen the relationship.

## Proactive engagement (the growth half)

Don't just react. Spend time daily engaging *outward*: comment meaningfully on the accounts your audience follows, on relevant hashtags/topics, on customers' and partners' posts, and on the "dream 100" accounts in the niche. Thoughtful outbound comments are a legitimate discovery and relationship channel — especially on LinkedIn, Instagram, and TikTok. Amplify UGC: reshare customers who tag the brand (with credit/permission), which rewards advocacy and produces free authentic content.

## Handling complaints and negativity

- **Acknowledge fast, publicly, without defensiveness.** "You're right to be frustrated — let's fix this." Take the detail to DM/email, but always respond publicly first so onlookers see the brand cares.
- **Never delete legitimate criticism** (it inflames and looks like a cover-up). Delete only spam, hate, or abuse per a documented moderation policy.
- **Don't feed trolls.** Distinguish an unhappy customer (engage, resolve) from a bad-faith troll (respond once factually, then disengage/hide). Hide, don't argue.
- **Know the escalation line:** coordinated attacks, legal/safety issues, exec-level or viral negative threads → escalate to the client immediately (see the crisis skill), don't improvise.

## Convert engagement to outcomes

Flag sales-intent DMs and comments and route them to the client's CRM/sales with context. Track recurring questions — they're content ideas and often signal a product/FAQ gap worth reporting to the client. Turn superfans into a program (early access, features, testimonials).

## Guardrails (always)

- Never share opinions on politics/religion or wade into unrelated controversy from the brand account.
- Never post private customer info; move account/order details to private channels.
- Never make promises (refunds, features, dates) you're not authorized to — say you'll check and follow up.
- Log anything sensitive and loop in the client.

## Quality bar

- Every genuine interaction gets a personalized, on-voice, timely reply.
- Complaints acknowledged publicly and resolved privately, never deleted.
- Daily outbound engagement, not just inbound reactions.
- Sales intent captured and routed; recurring questions surfaced to the client.
- Escalation triggers respected.

## Failure modes

- Slow or no replies → the #1 loyalty leak.
- Canned/robotic responses → reads as a bot, kills trust.
- Deleting or arguing with criticism → Streisand effect.
- Feeding trolls / matching hostility from the brand account.
- Purely reactive, zero outbound engagement → missing the growth half.
- Overstepping authority on refunds/promises.

**Great** looks like: fast, human, on-voice replies to every real interaction; complaints defused with public accountability and private resolution; consistent outbound engagement that seeds relationships; sales and product signals routed to the client; and a brand that feels like a person people want to talk to.
