# Delivery and Asset Management

The job isn't done when the edit looks good — it's done when the client has correctly-specced files that play everywhere they need them, the rights are clean, and everything is archived so you can find it in two years. Sloppy delivery undoes great creative: a hero video uploaded in the wrong codec that buffers, or a source file lost when the client asks for a re-cut.

## Know the destination specs before you export

Every platform has real constraints. Export to the destination, not to a generic "HD file."
- **Aspect ratio and resolution** per placement: 16:9 (YouTube, web, landscape), 9:16 (Reels, TikTok, Shorts, Stories), 1:1 or 4:5 (feed). Deliver each version reframed, not cropped blindly.
- **Codec and container:** H.264/H.265 in MP4 is the safe web/social delivery codec. Deliver a high-bitrate but reasonable file — social platforms re-compress on upload, so give them clean source (higher bitrate) to compress from. For broadcast or client re-edit, deliver a mezzanine (ProRes/DNxHD).
- **Frame rate:** match the source; don't let the export tool silently convert 24→30 and introduce judder.
- **Loudness:** normalize to the platform's spec (commonly around -14 LUFS integrated for streaming, -16 LUFS for web/podcast; confirm current spec). Wildly hot or quiet audio gets flagged or sounds amateur.
- **Duration/file-size caps:** confirm max length per placement (ad bumpers, Stories) and file-size limits.
- **Captions:** deliver burned-in captions for social plus a separate .srt sidecar for platforms that support selectable captions and for accessibility/SEO.

## The deliverables package

Assemble a clean, labeled package — not a pile of ambiguous exports:
- Final masters per platform, named with a clear convention: `client_project_placement_aspect_version_date` (e.g. `acme_launch_reels_9x16_v3_2026-08.mp4`).
- Thumbnail/poster frames if requested (a strong custom thumbnail drives play rate — offer it).
- Caption files (.srt).
- A short "how to use" note: which file goes on which platform, recommended upload settings, and the CTA/link to pair with each.
- Only the approved final versions in the client-facing folder. Keep work-in-progress out of it.

## Rights, licensing, and clearances

Deliver clean rights or you hand the client a liability:
- **Music:** confirm the license covers the client's actual use (platform, paid ads, territory, term). A "free for YouTube" track is not cleared for a TV spot or a paid social campaign. Keep the license receipt/ID and pass it to the client.
- **Stock footage/graphics/fonts:** confirm commercial license scope. Note any attribution requirements.
- **Talent/location releases:** confirm signed releases exist for everyone identifiable and every location. Flag any usage limits (e.g. "internal use only," "no paid media") on the release so the client doesn't violate them.
- **Rights summary:** deliver a one-line rights note per licensed asset. This is what protects the client (and you) later.

## Archive so future-you can re-cut

The single most common client re-engagement is "can you make a 15-second version" or "swap the pricing." If your project is lost, that's a rebuild.
- Archive the full project: editorial project file, all source media, audio, graphics assets, and licenses. Follow 3-2-1: at least two copies on different media, one offsite/cloud.
- Consolidate/collect the project (media-managed) so the archive is self-contained and won't break on relink.
- Keep a mezzanine master export in the archive independent of the editing software, so the final is recoverable even if the project won't open in a future software version.
- Record project metadata: shoot date, locations, talent, music licenses, key contacts, and where the archive lives. Note the retention period agreed with the client.

## Handoff and closeout

- Deliver via a proper link/drive with clear folder structure, not a chaotic dump.
- Walk the client through what each file is and confirm they can open and upload them — a delivery they can't use is a non-delivery.
- Offer the "future versions" note proactively: what re-cuts are cheap now because the project is fresh and archived.

## Quality bar

- **Great:** every placement has a correctly-specced, correctly-named, loudness-normalized file with captions; rights are documented and clean; the full project is archived 3-2-1 and re-openable; the client knows exactly what to upload where.
- **Failure:** one generic file for all platforms, wrong codec that buffers or gets rejected, missing captions, unlicensed music slipped through, or a lost project that can't be re-cut. Delivery specs and archiving are not optional polish — they are the deliverable.
