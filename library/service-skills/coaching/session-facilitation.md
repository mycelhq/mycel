# Session Facilitation

A coaching session is not a conversation that happens to be helpful — it's a structured hour that reliably produces a shift and a next action. The core discipline: the client does the thinking and owns the answer. Your job is to create the space, ask the questions, and hold them accountable — not to solve it for them. Solutions you hand over don't stick; insights they reach themselves do.

## The session arc (a reliable structure)

Use a consistent frame every session. The GROW model is the durable default:
1. **Goal for this session.** Open by asking what they want from this specific hour. "What would make this a good use of our time today?" A session without a chosen focus wanders. Let them pick — even if it's not what you'd choose.
2. **Reality.** Explore the current situation with real specifics. Get concrete examples, facts, and the client's own read. Resist jumping to solutions; most sessions under-explore reality and over-rush to action.
3. **Options.** Generate possibilities. Ask "what could you do?" and stay silent. Push past the first two obvious answers — the third and fourth are where the real options live. Only offer your own idea after theirs are exhausted, and offer it as one option among many ("one thing I've seen work is X — does that fit?"), never as the answer.
4. **Will / Way forward.** Convert to a specific commitment: what, by when, what's the first step, what might get in the way. Close every session with a concrete action the client chose.

Always open by following up on the prior session's commitment (see accountability skill) and close by locking the next one.

## Ask, don't tell

The skill is questions. Great coaching questions are:
- **Open, short, and forward.** "What's really going on here?" "What do you want?" "What's stopping you?" "What would you do if you weren't afraid?" One clean question beats a paragraph of setup.
- **Aimed at the client's thinking, not information for you.** You ask not to understand for yourself but to help them see. "What are you assuming that might not be true?"
- **Willing to sit in silence.** After a hard question, say nothing. The client's discomfort is where the insight forms. Filling the silence with a follow-up robs them of it. Count to ten before rescuing.

Avoid: leading questions that smuggle in your answer ("don't you think you should…"), stacked multi-part questions, and "why" questions that trigger defensiveness (prefer "what" and "how").

## Presence and listening

- **Listen at the deepest level** — not just words but the emotion, the energy shift, what's avoided. Notice when their voice changes, when they deflect, what they skip. Name it: "I noticed you moved past that quickly — what's there?"
- **Reflect and reframe.** Play back what you heard ("So what I'm hearing is…") to confirm and to let them hear themselves. Offer reframes that open new angles ("what if this isn't a time problem but a priorities problem?").
- **Be fully present.** No note-taking that pulls you out, no rehearsing your next question. The client feels the difference between being heard and being processed.
- **Follow the energy and the client's agenda**, not your plan. If something alive surfaces, go there — even if it wasn't the stated topic.

## Challenge with care

Coaching that only supports doesn't change anyone. Once trust is built:
- **Hold up the mirror.** Name patterns you see across sessions ("this is the third time avoiding this conversation came up"). Direct, kind, specific.
- **Challenge the story.** Question limiting beliefs and assumptions the client treats as fact.
- **Don't collude.** When the client rationalizes or dodges accountability, name it gently rather than letting it slide. Comfortable coaching is often useless coaching.
Balance: enough safety that they're honest, enough challenge that they grow. Read which the moment needs.

## Failure modes to avoid

- **Becoming the consultant** — dispensing advice and to-do lists. It feels productive and creates dependency, but the client learns nothing durable.
- **Over-exploring, never committing** — a lovely reflective session with no action. Every session ends in a commitment.
- **Rushing to solution** — skipping reality, solving the surface problem, missing the real one.
- **Rescuing from silence and discomfort** — the exact moments that produce breakthroughs.

## Quality bar

- **Great:** the client did most of the talking and nearly all the thinking, reached an insight in their own words, felt genuinely heard and appropriately challenged, and left with a specific self-chosen commitment. You asked far more than you told.
- **Failure:** you talked more than they did, handed them your solution, let the session drift without a commitment, or kept it comfortable and avoided the real issue.
