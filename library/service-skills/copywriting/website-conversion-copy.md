# Website & Conversion Copy

You are writing copy that has seconds to earn attention and one job per page: move the visitor to the next step. Marketing copy is not description — it's persuasion grounded in the reader's self-interest. Write to one specific person, about their problem, in their words.

## Research before writing — the copy lives in the research

Great conversion copy is assembled, not invented. Before writing, gather: the actual language customers use (mine reviews, support tickets, sales-call notes, competitor reviews on G2/Amazon/Reddit) — you want their exact phrases for pain and desired outcome, because copy in the customer's voice outperforms clever copy every time. Identify the **one core desire** and the **one biggest objection**. Understand where the reader is on awareness (unaware → problem-aware → solution-aware → product-aware → most-aware); a cold problem-aware visitor needs the problem named, a product-aware visitor needs the offer and proof. Copy that mismatches awareness stage falls flat.

## The hero — the only section most visitors read

The above-the-fold hero decides bounce vs. read-on. It must answer, instantly: what is this, who is it for, why should I care, what do I do next. Structure:

- **Headline**: lead with the visitor's desired outcome or their problem, not your company or a feature. "Get paid on time, every time" beats "AI-powered invoicing platform." Be specific and concrete; vague benefit claims ("Empower your business") are invisible. Numbers, specificity, and clarity beat cleverness — never sacrifice clarity for a pun.
- **Subhead**: one sentence that clarifies *how* / for whom, adding the mechanism or the specificity the headline earned.
- **Primary CTA**: one, action- and value-oriented ("Start collecting", "Get my free audit"), not "Submit." Repeat the same CTA down the page; don't split attention across competing asks.
- Optional: a trust signal (logo bar, metric, rating) visible without scrolling.

## Structure the page as an argument

Order sections to walk the reader from problem to action: (1) hook/problem, (2) the promise/outcome, (3) how it works (mechanism — make the transformation believable), (4) proof (testimonials, results, logos, data), (5) objection handling (FAQ, guarantees, risk reversal), (6) close/CTA. Every section earns the scroll to the next. One page, one conversion goal — a homepage can have a secondary path but must have a dominant one.

## Write benefit-led, feature-supported

Translate every feature to a benefit: feature → what it does → what that means for the reader's life ("256-bit encryption" → "your data is locked down" → "you sleep at night, no breach lawsuits"). Lead with the benefit, cite the feature as proof. But don't strand benefits without mechanism — modern readers are skeptical; "how" makes "what" believable.

## Craft at the sentence level

- **Clarity over cleverness, always.** If a reader has to re-read, you've lost. Grade-7 reading level for most B2C, still plain for B2B.
- Short sentences. Active voice. Second person ("you"), present tense. Cut hedges ("we believe", "helps to", "can potentially").
- Specificity is credibility: "join 12,000 freelancers" beats "join thousands." Real numbers, real names, real outcomes.
- Front-load. The first 3 words of a headline/button do the work.
- One idea per sentence, one message per section.

## Proof and risk reversal

Claims without proof are noise. Pair each major claim with evidence: a specific testimonial (with name/role/photo — anonymous quotes are weak), a stat, a case result, a recognizable logo. Handle the top objection explicitly — money-back guarantee, free trial, no credit card, cancel anytime — to lower the perceived risk of clicking.

## CTA discipline

The button copy states the value of clicking, not the mechanical act. Reduce friction language near it ("takes 2 minutes", "no card needed"). Don't bury the CTA or offer five equal ones. Make the desired action the path of least resistance.

## Quality bar

Acceptable: grammatical, on-topic, describes the offer. **Great**: a hero that in five seconds tells the right-awareness visitor what/who/why/next, headlines in the customer's own outcome-language, every feature translated to a benefit with mechanism, specific proof against every claim, the top objection dismantled, and one repeated value-stated CTA. Failure modes to reject: company-first headlines ("Welcome to Acme"), feature dumps with no benefit, vague inflated adjectives with no specifics, "Submit" buttons, anonymous testimonials, cleverness that costs clarity, and multiple competing CTAs. Read it as the skeptical, busy, self-interested stranger it's for — if it doesn't earn the next scroll, rewrite it.
